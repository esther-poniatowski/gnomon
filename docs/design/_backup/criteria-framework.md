
# Operational Framework for Representing Research Reasoning

## Problem and Objectives

The objective is to design a **structured operational framework** to represent **research reasoning**.

The architecture must be **necessary**, **sufficient**, and **optimal** for representing research reasoning in a structured operational framework.

Decisively, the system must not only represent epistemic content, but also represent the **logic of inquiry** that determines which objects are necessary for answering a given question.

The framework must provide the following benefits:

- **Local clarity**: each unit has a precise function
- **Global tractability**: relations can be indexed and queried

## Quality constraints

> [!INFO] Migrated to [cognitive manipulability](_framework-criteria#^t1-cognitive-manipulability) — the five-operation list (reconstruction, contrast, compression, transfer, diagnosis) is the F body. The downstream "no unsupported derived claim", "no blind step", "no globally irrelevant step", "no opaque transformation", and "no hidden branch choice" sub-bullets redirect to existing F's per the classification table.

A representation brings genuine understanding when it enables the following operations:

- **Reconstruction**: A reader can reconstruct the global strategy from local steps.
- **Contrast**: A reader can see why one route was chosen over another.
- **Compression**: A reader can summarize the argument by strategic blocks rather than by isolated formulas.
- **Transfer**: A reader can reuse the same strategic pattern in another problem because the method is made explicit.
- **Diagnosis**: A weak point can be identified as:
	- unsupported claim
	- missing condition
	- unjustified transition
	- irrelevant detour
	- hidden assumption
	- poor subquestion decomposition

These requirement suggest the following quality constraints on the representation:

- **No unsupported derived claim**: Every derived claim must have at least one incoming `supports` edge with an explicit warrant.
- **No blind step**: Every inferential step must have a local goal.
- **No globally irrelevant step**: Every inferential step must connect, directly or indirectly, to the root question through a typed relation (e.g. `answers`, `refines`, `supports`).
- **No opaque transformation**: Every non-trivial step must have a conceptual effect.
- **No hidden branch choice**: Whenever several admissible routes exist, the chosen route should include at least one rejected alternative.

These constraints are precisely what standard proof writing often fails to encode.

Concretely, this means that a reasoning system should adopt the following patterns:

### Encoding the conceptual effect of each step

Validity alone does not explain the function of a step. A dedicated field should specify the **conceptual effect**.

Typical effects include:

- introducing a usable representation
- eliminating irrelevant variables
- reducing a global claim to local obligations
- isolating an invariant
- exposing a contradiction
- transforming an empirical question into a formal criterion
- shifting from existence to construction
- shifting from semantics to syntax
- turning a vague claim into a testable subclaim

This field is crucial because many steps are understood through the transformation they perform on the problem space, not only through formal derivability.

### Encoding alternatives and rejections

Understanding is partly contrastive.

A reasoning becomes far clearer when a step indicates:

- which plausible alternatives existed
- why one route was chosen
- why other routes were discarded

This avoids the common defect of proofs that appear arbitrary despite being valid.

### Encoding the statuses of content

A reasoning system should not treat all statements as homogeneous. Distinguishing the epistemic status is critical in research contexts, where many chains of thought contain mixed epistemic statuses.

|Status|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**Assumption**|provisionally accepted premise|
|**Derived claim**|proposition established by explicit warrant|
|**Heuristic**|guiding idea without full proof|
|**Research hypothesis**|candidate explanation still under evaluation|
