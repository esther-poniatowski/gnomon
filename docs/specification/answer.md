# Answer record

> [!QUESTION] Goal: what does a researcher write in each field of an answer record, and when is each field required?

The [answer form](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md) states the grounds of each element.

An answer is one typed claim with its grounds. Each answer holds a **core**, whose elements every aim requires, and one **aim block**, which holds what the aim of its question requires. A model that serves two aims therefore grounds two answers, one per aim. The model itself, when the route of the answer runs on one, is a [record of its own](model.md#^model-record). ^answer-elements

| Element | When it is required | Content | Constraint on the element | Examples |
| ------- | ------------------- | ------- | ------------------------- | -------- |
| Claim | always | "[the claim of the answer]", as a reading, and, once formalized, an expression over declared symbols | - stated over the symbols of the model on which the route runs, or over those of the target and the phenomenon when the route runs on the target; <br>- a claim found but not yet formalized keeps its reading, and a caveat states the gap | "unequal amplification of the activity modes makes the population synchronous rather than asynchronous" |
| Grounds — what the claim depends on | always | per ground, its content, its *role*: `assumption` · `idealization` · `condition` · `datum` · `warrant` · `criterion`, and the record that holds it. A ground with the role `idealization` also states the distortion that it introduces, its [motive](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^idealizations) (tractability, isolation or limit), the gain that it provides, the range over which it holds, and whether it is removable (controlled, ineliminable or unknown) | - a law of the model or of the background enters with the role `warrant`; <br>- a version of the data enters with the role `datum`; <br>- a condition of the [domain](../epistemology/objects/subject/domain.md) enters with the role `condition`; <br>- a criterion of the question, such as a tolerance or a detection criterion, enters with the role `criterion`; <br>- a ground with the role `idealization` is an assumption absent from the [domain of conditions](../epistemology/objects/subject/domain.md#^analysis-assumptions) $D$, classified as an idealization of the target of the answer, as the [rule on idealizations](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^idealizations) states | the mean-field rate equations (warrant); the mean-field limit (idealization: limit, it closes the rate equations; controlled, since a finite-size correction is known; valid for a large number of neurons); constant drive (condition); the recordings of version 3 (datum) |
| Abstractions | when the claim leaves out a factor of the target and claims nothing about it | per factor, the factor left out | - not a ground, since an abstraction asserts nothing; <br>- only a factor that the content of the claim does not mention; a reason asserting that the factor makes no difference to the claimed quantity is a ground with the role `assumption` | the axonal delays of a rate model whose claims bear only on stationary rates; [abstraction and the truth test](../epistemology/vocabulary/representations/idealization-and-abstraction.md#^truth-test) |
| Dependence — how the claim depends on its grounds | always | per entry, a minimal set of grounds that suffices for the claim; the result of a sensitivity analysis over one ground [saltelli2008]; or the result of a robustness analysis across models that share the claimed structure [weisberg2006] | - each entry names grounds of this answer; <br>- a minimal sufficient set states what the derivation requires, not that the claim fails without it, which only a sensitivity or a robustness result shows | the derivation of the claim requires the mean-field limit and the delay (minimal sufficient set) |
| Route | always | `derivation` · `proof` · `simulation` · `estimation` · `measurement`, the records on which it runs (the target, one model, or a family of models), and a pointer to the derivation, the code or the data | - the route decides whether the answer needs a [binding](../epistemology/objects/answer/model-and-binding.md#^when-a-binding); <br>- the internal steps of a derivation stay in the derivation | the linear stability analysis of the rate model (derivation, on one model) |
| Domain | always | the systems and the conditions over which the claim holds | - for a deductive route, *derived* from the grounds with the roles `assumption` and `condition`, and from the range that each ground with the role `idealization` states, not stated; <br>- for a route that runs on a model other than the target, bounded by the domain of comparison of the binding | the networks with more than a thousand neurons, under constant drive |
| Variant | when the members of the target declare different elements, and the answer accounts for only a subset of the class | the variant of the target that the answer accounts for, and one further variant for each of its parts with a narrower scope | — | the community that models its resources explicitly, where the account rests on the pools; member schemas under one reference class [lundberg2021] |
| Status — the attitude taken toward the answer | always | entertained · hypothesized · accepted · retracted | — | a mechanism proposed for pursuit (hypothesized); a theorem with a proof checked by the assessment (accepted); [attitude and support](../epistemology/vocabulary/representations/claim-and-hypothesis.md#^attitude-support) |
| Support — the kind of evidence for the answer as a whole | always | unsupported · informal · formally-proven · empirically-supported | — | an account drawn from simulations of toy models (informal); a published theorem (formally-proven) |
| Uncertainty | when the claim assigns a value | the uncertainty of each value, as an interval, a distribution or a bound | — | a frequency of 40 ± 3 Hz |
| Modal strength — the strength that the answer claims | always | actual · possible · necessary | — | an account of how the trained networks of the class produce the delay (actual); a model that shows one way in which the delay can arise (possible); [modal strength](../epistemology/objects/task/modal-strength.md) |
| Erotetic relation — how the answer stands to its question | always | `complete` · `partial` · `corrective` · `reformulating`, with the presupposition that a corrective answer denies, or the revised question that a reformulating answer answers | see the [erotetic relation](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^erotetic-relation) | the derivation of the delay for one class of initializations only (partial) |
| Binding — the record through which the claim bears on the target | when the route runs on a model other than the target, and the claim bears on the target | per model, the identifier and the version of one [binding](binding.md#^binding-record) of that model to the phenomenon of the question | one binding per model of a family | version 2 of the binding of the rate model to the gamma oscillations |
| Use — the purpose of an answer whose claim bears on no target | when the claim bears only on a model or on a formal class, as the [declared use](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^declared-use) states | a use such as `tool`, `proof-of-principle`, `conceptual-exploration` or `understanding`, with the property that the model must have for that use | the list of uses is open; a new use states the property that it requires | a toy model showing that a delay can arise without any change of the data (proof of principle: the model must exhibit the delay) |
| Aim block | when the erotetic relation is `complete` or `partial` | the block of the [aim](#^aim-blocks) of the question | one block, of the aim of the question | the explanatory block of an account of the oscillation |
| Relations — the links to other answers | when the answer bears on another answer | per link, the other answer and the relation: `supports` · `presupposes` · `tests` · `defeats` · `revises` | see the [relations between answers](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^answer-relations) | a prediction that tests an explanation (tests); a corrected account (revises) |

- **One rule decides where a limit is recorded.** When the reference class of the question is the limit system itself, the limit is a restriction of the [domain](../epistemology/objects/subject/domain.md), written in the field `limit` of the question. Otherwise, the limit is an idealization with the motive `limit`, recorded as a ground of the answer under the rule above, and its field `removable` states whether a finite-size correction is known. The same limit of one model can therefore be an idealization for one question and a restriction of the domain for another. The [rule on limits](../epistemology/vocabulary/representations/idealization-and-abstraction.md#^limit-rule) gives the grounds. ^limit-rule

- **The status of an answer states its attitude, and the field `support` states the kind of evidence for it.** Both fields bear on the answer as a whole, whereas each law of the model keeps its own warrant. The values of the two fields, and their independence, are defined with the [attitudes and supports](../epistemology/vocabulary/representations/claim-and-hypothesis.md#^attitude-support). ^status-and-support

- **An answer names the members that it accounts for.** A class may hold members that declare different elements. An answer resting on a quantity that only some members carry accounts *only* for that subset. Such an answer names the variant that it accounts for. Each of its parts with a narrower scope names its own variant as well. A target system [declares its variants](target-system.md#^variants) by the same rule, as does [each entry of a phenomenon](phenomenon.md#^entries-hold-of-members). An answer naming no variant accounts for every member, so it may rest only on quantities that all of them carry. ^answer-names-its-members

- **A candidate answer records its abstractions apart from its idealizations.** The element Abstractions of an [answer](#^answer-elements) makes the [truth test](../epistemology/vocabulary/representations/idealization-and-abstraction.md#^truth-test) operative: the answer asserts nothing about a factor listed among its abstractions, whereas an omission read as complete is a distortion and belongs to the idealizations.

## Aim blocks

Each aim of the [registry of aims](../epistemology/objects/task/epistemic-aim.md#^aim-registry) whose category generates a block has one block. The answer holds the block of the aim of its question, and the block holds what the [answer conditions](../epistemology/objects/task/epistemic-aim.md#^answer-conditions) of that aim require of a direct answer. ^aim-blocks

| Aim | The block holds | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `description` | the values or the characterization of the explanandum variables and of the features of the named phenomenon, at the grain of the question, per source of variation with its quantification. A value obtained by an estimation on the target is the estimate that the [estimator of the phenomenon](../epistemology/objects/subject/phenomenon.md#^phenomenon-owns-estimation) computes, cited with its version of the data; a characterization that no estimator computes, such as a structure derived from the admitted laws, is stated by the answer | the frequency and the spatial extent of the gamma oscillations during visual stimulation |
| `exploration` | the space explored: the observables and the varied factors with their ranges; the equivalence criterion that partitions the space; each of the two marked as an *input*, fixed by the question, or an *output*, produced by the answer; and the classes found, each with its pattern and the result of the [detection criterion](../epistemology/objects/subject/phenomenon.md#^exploratory-phenomenon) | the collective states of a network as the ratio of excitation to inhibition varies, partitioned by the sign of the leading eigenvalue (criterion as input) |
| `prediction` | per predicted output, the output among the outputs to match that the task names, the condition of the target under which it is predicted, and its value or distribution | the gamma power when the stimulus contrast doubles |
| `explanation` | the contrastive explanandum, the explanatory claim, the relation and the factors, as the [block of an explanation](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^explanation-block) states | the account of the oscillation by an inhibitory delay |
| `modal-knowledge` | the modal claim (possible, impossible or necessary), the space of alternatives that the admitted laws and the domain fix, and the witness of a possibility or the proof of an impossibility or a necessity | a network without inhibitory neurons that oscillates at gamma frequency (witness) |
| `model-knowledge` | the consequence (a derived law, a regime, a limit or a structural property) or the counterexample, with the proof obligations of the task that it discharges | the uniqueness of the fixed point of a rate network below a critical gain |

### The block of an explanation

The fields below hold the elements that make the dependences of the core explanatory, as the [block of an explanation](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^explanation-block) states.

| Specification | When it is required | Content | Examples |
| ------------- | ------------------- | ------- | -------- |
| Contrastive explanandum | always | the fact and the foils that the explanation separates, read from the [contrast](../epistemology/objects/subject/contrast.md) of the question, or the fact alone when its locus is `none` | synchronous oscillations rather than asynchronous activity |
| Explanatory claim | always | the claim that named dependences of the core are explanatory: they make the difference between the fact and its foils, or they make the fact arise | the dependence of the oscillation on the delay is explanatory; its dependence on the drive is not |
| Relation | always | the type of the relation, one of the [requested relations](../epistemology/objects/task/requested-relation.md) | mathematical; causal-mechanistic |
| Factors $E$ | always | per factor, its claim: a *condition* on a quantity, a *property* of a structure or of a law, or a feature of the *organization*; the element on which it bears; the laws and relations that carry its effect; the explanandum variable or manifestation that it accounts for; the foils that it rules out; and the external systems on which it draws | the synaptic delay exceeds its critical value (condition; denoting); the connectivity has rank two (property of a structure; coarse-graining) |
| Factors: target status | always | `denoting` · `coarse-graining` · `limit-construct` · `structural-constraint`, as the [target status of a factor](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^target-status) states; not applicable under a declared use | the delay (denoting); the infinite size of a mean-field network (limit construct) |

- A factor is often a property found by the answer and absent from every earlier record, as the [account of a factor as a property](../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md#^factor-may-be-found) states. The property is then declared in the model: a property of a law as a law of the model, and a property of a structure as a relation of its organization. A quantity that the property requires is declared in the model as well. The factor then bears on the carrier of the property: a single element, or a family of elements. The field `holds`, in turn, states the property itself. When the property is an invariance, the field `under` names the transformation group. *Example*: the [geometry of a representation](../epistemology/vocabulary/representations/representation-and-encoding.md) is invariant under the rotations of the state space. Where the answer has found the property but cannot yet formalize it, `holds` stays empty and a caveat states the gap. The record then keeps the finding without presenting it as a formula.
