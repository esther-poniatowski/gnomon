# Choosing the next act

> [!QUESTION] Goal: once a problem is stated, which act of research comes next, and on what grounds?

Once a problem is stated, the next act is chosen in five steps:

1. **Questions.** List the questions to diagnose: the associated question of the problem, the questions that its obstacles generate, and the questions on which a condition depends across questions.
2. **Diagnosis.** State each [condition for obtaining an answer](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md) of each question as established, not established or refuted, and find the open conditions.
3. **Acts.** Map each open condition to the activities that act on it, or turn it into a new question. Map each refuted condition to a revision. Once every condition of a route holds, the acts that obtain the answer come next, followed by the [evaluation](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^evaluation) of the answer.
4. **Readiness.** Keep the acts whose inputs exist and are warranted.
5. **Ranking.** Choose among the acts kept.

Each step either returns a determinate output or presents alternatives between which the researcher chooses. The choices are the following:

- which question record stands for the associated question, when a record narrows the demand of the problem;
- the judgments that fix axis A, such as whether a datum is improbable under the background, or whether the support of an accepted claim is weaker than the demand requires;
- which question a conflict generates;
- whether a candidate that fails its verdict is withdrawn or revised;
- whether a construction route is named;
- whether a condition is asserted as established or as refuted, including the judgment that a definition is adequate to its use;
- the finite set of means considered, which separates a refutation for lack of means from a condition not established;
- whether a provisional answer is adopted for a dependency on another question;
- whether an open condition is pursued through the activities that act on it, or turned into a question;
- which revision of a refuted condition is taken, when its row offers several;
- how the inputs of an act are bound, when several records fit;
- whether an unsupported claim is admitted as a working assumption under the warrant `posited`;
- whether the acts that rest on a verdict not yet accepted are run before its acceptance;
- when the main question is re-examined;
- the ranking of step 5.

Choosing an act that reduces the difference between the present records and the solution of the problem is an instance of means–ends analysis [newell1972]. The steps, their rules and their combination are a proposal of this method, and the sources cited supply their parts. The activity map below is checked against the [model of the conditions](../../tests/test_gnomon/answerability_model.yml) by a [test](../../tests/test_gnomon/test_answerability_model.py), which also checks each quoted norm against the note of its activity.

## Step 1: the questions to diagnose

The demand of the problem, its request included, is a given of the problem, and that demand fixes the [associated question](definitions/epistemic-problem.md#^associated-question) $Q_\pi$. The obstacles of the problem form a set of values of axis A, diagnosed relative to that demand, and each obstacle generates the questions listed among the [questions generated by each obstacle](definitions/problem-types.md#^obstacle-questions). The questions to diagnose are $Q_\pi$, the generated questions, and the questions on which a diagnosed condition depends across questions, unless a presupposition of $Q_\pi$ is refuted outright, in which case the problem is dissolved. ^questions-to-diagnose

- **A conflict generates a choice.** A conflict can generate a question of withdrawal or a question of explanation, and the researcher chooses which to pose.
- **A negative verdict leads to a revision.** A question of verdict is answered once its verdict is obtained and accepted, and the acceptance of a verdict rests on the judgment of the norm of the step that produced it. When the verdict obtained for a candidate is negative, three acts follow in order. First, the norm of the producing step is judged, if it is pending. Next, the verdict is accepted. Finally, the candidate is withdrawn by `belief-revision`, or revised, as the researcher chooses. A withdrawn candidate, or a revision identical to it, returns only with a recorded new ground, such as an overturned verdict or new evidence, so the record of withdrawn candidates keeps the ground of each withdrawal. When no candidate remains, the obstacle becomes an open gap, unless the question presupposed that its candidates were complete, in which case its soundness is refuted.
- **An unwarranted claim stays admitted with its warrant.** A claim accepted with a warrant weaker than the demand requires stays admitted with the warrant that it has, such as `fitted`, and serves as a premise of other work, each result resting on it staying conditional on it. This pragmatic acceptance adopts the claim as a premise without claiming more for it [cohen1992]. The claim with the warrant that the demand requires, such as a law derived from the admitted laws, becomes the candidate of the question of verdict. A claim without any support enters the background only by a recorded decision of the researcher, as a working assumption under the warrant `posited`. Such a decision establishes no fact, so its result is a revisable assumption, as the [terminal acts](question-decomposition.md#^terminal-acts) state.
- **A question of explication judges by adequacy.** Its candidates are candidate criteria, judged by their adequacy to the explicandum, not by truth [carnap1950_logical]. For such a question, the conditions of discrimination and bridging are read with adequacy as the standard, and the explicandum is not a slot of the question.

## Step 2: diagnose the open conditions

The conditions of a question are diagnosed on each of its routes. The route of selection is always diagnosed. The [route of construction](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^routes) is diagnosed when a procedure is named that would yield a direct answer. Definiteness is diagnosed term by term. ^open-condition

**Rule: work on an open condition.** A condition is *open* when its state is *not established* and every condition on which it [depends](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^condition-dependencies) is in the state *established*. A dependency on another question is met as the conditions of answerability state: through the soundness of the other question, for the link `presupposes`, and otherwise through an accepted or a provisional answer. A refuted condition is handled by a revision in step 3.

- **The diagnosis does not stall on the order.** Within one question, the dependencies have no cycle. Therefore, when no condition of a route is refuted, either all its conditions are established or at least one is open. Across questions, a condition can wait on another question, which step 1 then lists among the questions to diagnose. A cycle of such waits is broken by adopting a provisional answer. In particular, the condition of candidates is open as soon as the answer type is established, even when no candidate has been proposed.
- **A condition can be asserted.** When the records hold no field for a condition, the researcher can state the condition as established or as refuted, with pointers to the records or the publications that show it. The [types of problem](definitions/problem-types.md) treat a conflict with a publication in the same way.
- **Once every condition of a route is established, the acts that obtain the answer become ready.** The acts that exercise access obtain the quantity, a combination step composes the answer through the bridging inference, and the evaluation judges the obtained answer. An interrogative act that obtains the answer to the question requires the soundness of that question, as the [rule on interrogative moves](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^condition-dependencies) states.

## Step 3: act on each condition

### An open condition

The table lists, for each condition, the activities of the [registry](../../src/gnomon/data/registries/activities.yml) that act on it, and marks as a *gap* each act that no registered activity types yet. An act moves a condition to the state *established* only through the record of its outcome, as the [states of a condition](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^condition-states) require: its norm has passed, its outcome is positive, and that outcome has been accepted. ^condition-activities

| Condition | Activities that act on it | Gaps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definiteness | `explication`, `explicit-definition`, `operationalization`, `concept-revision`; `delimitation` and `formalization`, which write formal declarations | no activity produces a first concept from an explicandum; no activity raises a phenomenon to commitment `specified` |
| Answer type | `question-formulation`, which types the answer space; `delimitation`, which fixes the extent through the target | no activity fills or revises the fields of the epistemic task |
| Soundness | `phenomenon-detection`, for the topic presupposition; `derivation`, for an existence presupposition or a formal presupposition | no activity records a negative detection |
| Candidates | `abduction`, `induction`, `analogical-inference`, `causal-inference`, `decomposition-localization`, `model-analogy`, which propose candidates; `de-idealization`, `idealization`, `abstraction`, `formalization`, `fitting`, which revise a candidate; `derivation`, whose consequence enters a candidate as a law with a derived warrant; `critique`, which names objections; `admissibility-check`, which applies the admissibility tests | no activity builds a model from a target specification and a background without a phenomenon; no activity retrieves an established result as a candidate; no activity derives from a candidate together with the admitted laws, since `derivation` takes one record |
| Discrimination | `experimental-design`, whose norm requires that the possible outcomes discriminate the rival answers; `derivation`, `simulation`, `prediction`, `thought-experiment`, each supplying the value of a quantity under one candidate | no activity compares the consequences of rival candidates held as separate records |
| Access | `experimental-design`, whose design fixes the procedure; `operationalization` | no activity develops a proof technique or an algorithm |
| Bridging | `derivation` of a linking lemma; `background-appraisal`, which admits an auxiliary law; `operationalization`, whose scale a measurement model then validates | — |
| Representation | `validation`, which judges a model adequate for the purpose of the question; `robustness-analysis`, `de-idealization`, `transfer`, `prediction`, and `testing` of a prediction of the model against the target | — |

Once every condition of a route is established, the acts that obtain and accept the answer come next:

- the acts that exercise access: `measurement`, `observation`, `intervention`, `simulation`, and `derivation` along the route of construction;
- the acts that compose and evaluate the answer: `testing`, `confirmation`, `model-selection`, `inference-to-the-best-explanation`, `validation`, `critique`; `admissibility-check`, along the route of construction; `explanation`, `classification`, `unification`, `reduction` or `integration`, when the demand asks for their output type;
- the act that changes the status of the records: `belief-revision`, which takes the verdict of the evaluation.

Further gaps concern every route:

- no activity accepts a derived consequence, since `belief-revision` takes only a verdict, a credence or a ranking;
- no activity turns a derived consequence into a phenomenon;
- no activity accepts a verdict, since the acceptance rests on the judgment of the norm of the step that produced it, and no activity judges the norm of a plan step;
- a question of explication has no record form, since its explicandum is not a slot.

**An open condition can become a question.** The new question asks for the object that the condition lacks. The note on [question decomposition](question-decomposition.md#^serving-sources) states this move as the first source of serving questions. Such a question does not serve the problem in the sense of erotetic implication, since its answers, such as a procedure of measurement, do not narrow the answers of $Q_\pi$. Therefore, `question-decomposition`, whose norm requires erotetic implication, does not produce it. The new question is linked to the question it serves by `enables`, among the [links between questions](definitions/problem-and-question.md#^linked-questions), and no registered activity produces it yet. A demand that is a conjunction is decomposed by `question-decomposition`, with one question per conjunct linked by `subquestion-of`. When $Q_\pi$ has no question record yet, `question-formulation` first states a question schema, and `subject-identification` raises its slots, so that the record becomes a question. ^condition-to-question

### A refuted condition

A refuted condition calls for a revision, and the researcher chooses among the revisions of its row. ^refuted-condition

| Refuted condition | Revisions |
| --- | --- |
| Soundness of $Q_\pi$ | refuted outright: the problem is dissolved, as the [outcomes of a problem](definitions/epistemic-problem.md#^problem-outcomes) state; refuted for lack of means: the presupposition becomes a question linked to $Q_\pi$ by `enables`, and the problem stands |
| Soundness of a generated question | the generated question is revised, or abandoned while the problem stands |
| Definiteness, when no adequate criterion exists | the term is sharpened when it is vague, replaced by a concept that separates the situations which it conflated, or abandoned when its extension is empty |
| Answer type, when no answer can meet it | the epistemic task is revised, for instance by a weaker modal strength or a coarser accuracy |
| Discrimination | the contrast or the domain of the question is narrowed; its resolution is coarsened so that the inseparable candidates form one answer; or the development of a new quantity or instrument becomes a question |
| Access | the other route is taken, or the development of a procedure becomes a question |
| Bridging | another quantity is chosen, or the missing auxiliary hypothesis becomes a question |
| Representation | the reference class or the domain of the question is restricted, or access is moved to a model that represents the target, or to the target itself |

The condition of candidates has no row. When all candidates fail the admissibility tests or are eliminated, the condition is not established, and its acts are those of an open condition. When the question presupposed that its candidates were complete, the elimination of all of them refutes that presupposition, and the row of soundness applies.

No registered activity revises a question, narrows its domain or its contrast, or decomposes a problem into subproblems. The note on [question decomposition](question-decomposition.md#^global-revision) names three revisions of a question: disambiguation, restriction of scope, and replacement of a concept. After any revision that changes a diagnosed question, the procedure restarts from step 1. Apart from any refutation, the main question is also re-examined at intervals, to find whether it needs one of these three revisions.

## Step 4: readiness of an act

An **act instance** is an activity whose inputs are bound to records, to the outputs of steps, or, for a `source`, to pointers. The condition served by the instance guides the binding. ^ready-act

- **An input must exist.** When an input does not exist, the search proceeds backward through the registry: the missing input becomes a subgoal, and the activities that produce it are examined in turn.
- **An input must be warranted.** A record lacks warrant when a step of the plan that produced it failed its norm, or when such a step took as input a record that lacks warrant. Therefore, the lack of warrant propagates from producer to consumer along the steps of the plan, whatever the output types of the steps. An act that uses a record without warrant inherits the failure, so the repair of the failed step becomes a subgoal, ranked before the act. A pending verdict propagates in the same way, and the result of an act that rests on it stays conditional on it. Readiness reads only the steps that the plan records.
- **A pending norm stays to be judged.** When the record produced by a step is stabilized while the verdict of the step is pending, the norm of the step remains to be judged, and the record is treated as the product of a pending step until then.
- **The necessity of a subgoal is read over plans.** A missing input is necessary for the goal when every plan that reaches the goal contains an act instance that requires it. A particular act that produces that input is necessary only when, in addition, no other activity produces the input. Even a necessary subgoal does not reach the goal, since the remaining steps are activities whose norms can fail. The problem reduces to the problem of producing that input only when the remaining steps form a recorded, uniform method, as the definition of [reduction](definitions/epistemic-problem.md#^reduction) requires. Hierarchical task-network planning decomposes a task by methods that replace it with subtasks [erol1994]. Erotetic search scenarios start from a principal question and lead, through auxiliary questions, to its answers [wisniewski2013].

## Step 5: ranking

Before the ranking, each act receives one of three statuses by the [test of relevance](question-decomposition.md#^relevance): necessary, useful or decorative. Decorative acts are removed, and the necessary and useful acts are compared by the first five criteria below. The sixth criterion orders the open conditions of one route, not the acts. ^ranking-criteria

| Criterion | Preferred | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnostic power | the act whose outcome most constrains the admissible answers, by eliminating candidates, refuting an assumption, or forcing a reformulation of the question | [lindley1956]; [chaloner1995] |
| Least favorable outcome | the act whose least favorable plausible outcome eliminates the largest share of the live candidates | [wald1950], with the departure stated below |
| Structural centrality | the act that bears on a load-bearing assumption, concept or mechanism, whose failure would restructure the problem | — |
| Feasibility | the act that the available means, or a simplified setting, allow to complete now, with the higher probability of completion and at the lower cost | this method |
| Strength of warrant | the act that delivers the stronger warrant for the answer | this method |
| Order of tests | among the open conditions of one route, the condition whose test has the lowest ratio of cost to probability of refutation | [unluyurt2025] |

- **A decisive negative result carries the most weight, together with its auxiliary hypotheses.** Confirmation accumulates slowly, whereas one decisive negative result can eliminate a structural hypothesis. Therefore, the criteria of diagnostic power and of the least favorable outcome favor acts designed as potential refutations of an assumption, as Popper's severe tests are [popper1963]. A negative result refutes a candidate only together with the auxiliary hypotheses of the bridging inference, and the refutation may indict these hypotheses instead [duhem1906].
- **Expected information gain measures diagnostic power over candidates.** Lindley measures the information that an experiment provides by the expected reduction of the uncertainty over the parameters or hypotheses [lindley1956]. Bayesian experimental design maximizes an expected utility, and Lindley's information is one choice of that utility [chaloner1995]. The measure is defined over candidates already proposed, so it does not capture the outcome that forces a reformulation.
- **The least favorable outcome departs from Wald's rule.** Wald's maximin rule ranges over all states [wald1950], whereas the criterion ranges over the plausible outcomes only. This restriction is a departure from the rule, adopted so that an implausible outcome does not decide the ranking.
- **The order of tests is a heuristic.** For a series system, where every test must pass, testing in increasing ratio of cost to probability of failure minimizes the expected cost, a result due to Butterworth [unluyurt2025]. The result assumes tests with two outcomes, fixed costs, independent outcomes, and a stop at the first failure. The conditions of a route meet none of these assumptions exactly: an act can leave a condition not established, a refutation for lack of means is revisable, and correlated conditions such as discrimination and access are not independent. The dependencies between conditions do not constrain the sort, since the open conditions have no unmet dependency among themselves. Sorting the open conditions by the ratio is therefore a heuristic, analogous to the fail-first principle of search over constraints [haralick1980].
- **Feasibility and the order of tests use different probabilities.** Feasibility uses the probability that an act completes. The order of tests uses the probability that a condition is refuted.
- **The criteria can conflict.** A feasible act can have a low diagnostic power. One rule settles the main conflict: prefer the act that is both highly constraining and reasonably tractable. Beyond that rule, the researcher weighs the criteria, and the first act whose value reaches a level stated in advance can be accepted without a search for the best one, as satisficing allows [simon1956].

Four heuristics propose acts instead of ranking them:

- **Problem solving.** Look at the unknown; find a related problem already solved; solve a part of the problem; specialize or generalize; work backward [polya1945].
- **Positive heuristic.** A research programme plans a sequence of models, from an idealized model to corrected models [lakatos1970]. *Example*: a mean-field limit followed by its finite-size corrections, each step typed by `de-idealization`.
- **Discovery of mechanisms.** Schema instantiation, modular subassembly, forward chaining from the causes, and backward chaining from the effects [craver2013].
- **Strategic rules.** The interrogative model separates its definitory rules, which state the admissible moves, from its strategic rules, which state the good moves [hintikka1999]. A deductive step can establish the presupposition of a question, and thereby allow the interrogative moves that obtain its answer.

## The acts named in practice

- **Raising a placeholder.** A slot is raised to `identified` when the question is posed, since a question names each referent. A slot is raised to `specified` when an open condition needs the definiteness of its term: the answer type, soundness, or access along the route of construction. A candidate stated in words only can be proposed, but it cannot pass the admissibility tests, which require references to declared constituents and quantities. A candidate stated in the [answer form](answer-form.md) needs `specified` slots, since the answer form refers to declared symbols.
- **Refining the question.** A question is refined by the revisions of step 3: when the soundness of a generated question, the answer type, discrimination or representation is refuted, or when a concept is replaced after definiteness is refuted.
- **Specifying the form of the answer.** The answer type is one of the conditions, and the epistemic task fixes it as far as its fields are filled. A field can be left open on purpose and narrowed later by a recorded revision. The proof obligations of the task bind the bridging inference, and its required accuracy binds representation. The tests of success are drawn from the specifications of the question, as the [conditions of satisfaction](satisfaction-conditions.md) state.
- **Decomposing.** A question is decomposed by `question-decomposition` when its demand is a conjunction. An open condition can also become a question, without an activity that types the move.
- **Deriving new claims.** A derivation acts on soundness for an existence presupposition or a formal presupposition, on discrimination through the consequences of one candidate, on bridging through a linking lemma, and on the candidates through a derived law. Along the route of construction, a derivation exercises access.

## Dependencies between acts

The dependencies between acts form cycles among types: a concept serves a question, the question selects a model, and the model can revise the concept. These cycles are resolved by iteration, under the conditions of [controlled iteration](specification-order.md#^controlled-iteration). Within the procedure, an iteration starts in three places: a refuted condition triggers a revision, a withdrawn record removes the warrant of the acts that used it, and a provisional answer to another question is later revised. The [register of serving questions](question-decomposition.md#^register) tracks withdrawn records by marking each serving question with the structural hypothesis on which it is conditioned, so that a refuted hypothesis invalidates the sub-questions that rest on it.

## Example

The facts below are those of the [records of the inquiry into delayed generalization](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/_index.md). The [problem](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/problem.yml) demands the determinants of the delay between fitting and generalization, a derivation of its duration, and its variation across random initializations. One [candidate answer](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/answer.yml) has status `hypothesized`: the delay results from the slow structuring of the embedding representation. The question record `quest.grokking.explain_delay` addresses the problem, and the example takes it as the record of $Q_\pi$. This choice narrows the demand, since the record adds a contrast, the relation `mathematical` and the modal strength `actual`.

1. **Questions.** No direct answer is accepted, so the problem exists. The set $H$ holds one candidate and none is accepted, which axis A records as *unassessed candidates*, whether or not an assessment exists. Whether the background evokes $Q_\pi$, so that the problem has an epistemic gap, is the existence part of soundness, diagnosed in step 2. The questions to diagnose are $Q_\pi$ and the question of verdict on the candidate.
2. **Diagnosis.**
    - *The verdict.* The [assessment](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/assessment.yml) of the candidate is stabilized, and the assessment records `fail` for the admissibility tests of scope, grain and product, and for the warrant tests of closure, fidelity and robustness, while its admissibility test of admissible explanans is pending. In the [plan](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/plan.yml), the steps `check` and `test` that produced the assessment, by `admissibility-check` and by `testing`, both record the verdict `pending`. The negative verdict is therefore obtained, and it is not yet accepted.
    - *$Q_\pi$.* The answer type holds component by component: the aim `explanation`, the relation `mathematical` and the modal strength `actual` are filled in the question record, and the admissible explanans is left open on purpose. The admissibility test that reads it stays `pending`, so the condition of candidates can be opened but not established before that component is narrowed. The topic part of soundness is established by a record field: the [phenomenon](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/phenomenon.yml) has the evidential status `established`. The existence part, that an account of the demanded type exists, is not established, since no record shows it, and the components that fix the type of result are filled, so the part is open. The condition of candidates is not established, since no admissibility verdict on the candidate has been accepted with a positive outcome. The assessment records three failures among these tests. The admissibility test of scope fails because the account is derived for a toy architecture, whereas the question ranges over finite overparameterized networks. The admissibility test of grain fails because the account does not derive the distribution of the delay across initializations. The evidence for the admissibility test of product cites both the class and the distribution. The admissibility test of modal strength passes, since the candidate claims the actual strength that the question requests, and whether that claim holds falls to the warrant tests, among which closure and fidelity fail. The answer type counts as fixed for the condition of candidates, so that condition is open.
3. **Acts.**
    - *The verdict, in order.* The norms of the steps `check` and `test` are judged first, an act that no registered activity types. The verdict is then accepted, and the candidate is withdrawn by `belief-revision` or revised, as the researcher chooses.
    - *The soundness of $Q_\pi$.* The existence part is open. The registry maps `derivation` to it, for a proof that an account of the demanded type exists, and no such proof is in view, so the researcher can assert the part with pointers, or pose it as a question linked to $Q_\pi$ by `enables`.
    - *The candidates of $Q_\pi$.* One act proposes a rival: `abduction`, which takes the phenomenon record, and it does not rest on the verdict. Two acts would revise the candidate, so they are the third act of the rule on negative verdicts: they follow the acceptance of the verdict, and running them before it is a choice listed above, their results then staying conditional on the verdict. For the class, `model-analogy` takes the toy model as its source, under the norm that the mapping preserves the relations relevant to the phenomenon and that no known difference defeats the transfer. `de-idealization` does not fit, since its norm requires a known correction to the idealized result, and no correction from the toy architecture to the class is known. For the distribution, a derivation must combine the candidate with the law of initialization, which the [background](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/background.yml) admits and the answer record does not state, and no registered activity derives from a candidate together with the admitted laws.
4. **Readiness.**
    - `abduction` binds the phenomenon record. That record was produced by the step `identify`, whose input descends from the steps `formulate` and `pose`, and all three verdicts are `pass`, so the act is ready. The plan records no step that raised the phenomenon to `specified`, so readiness reads only these verdicts.
    - `model-analogy` binds the answer record as its source model. That record was produced by the steps `propose` and `derive`, with the verdicts `pass` and `pending`, so the act is ready, and its result stays conditional on the verdict of `derive`.
    - The derivation of the distribution is blocked twice: its activity is a gap, and the background record was produced by the step `admit`, whose verdict is `fail`, so the repair of that step is a subgoal ranked before it.
5. **Ranking.** Within the verdict, the order is fixed by the rule on negative verdicts. The order of `abduction` relative to the acts on the verdict is left to the researcher, by the criteria of step 5, whereas the two revisions of the candidate follow the acceptance of the verdict, unless the researcher runs them before it, conditionally. Within the revision for the distribution, the repair of the step `admit` precedes the derivation by the rule of readiness, not by the ranking.
