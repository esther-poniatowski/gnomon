# Choosing the next act

> [!QUESTION] Goal: once a problem is stated, which act of research comes next, and on what grounds?

Once a problem is stated, the next act is chosen in five steps:

1. **Questions.** List the questions to diagnose: the associated question of the problem, and the questions that its obstacles generate.
2. **Diagnosis.** State each [condition of answerability](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md) of each question as established, not established or refuted, and find the open conditions.
3. **Acts.** Map each open condition to the acts that establish it or contribute to it, or turn it into a new question. Map each refuted condition to a revision.
4. **Readiness.** Keep the acts whose inputs exist and are warranted.
5. **Ranking.** Choose among the acts kept.

Each step either returns a determinate output or presents alternatives between which the researcher chooses. The choices are the following:

- which question a conflict generates;
- whether a candidate that fails its verdict is withdrawn or revised;
- whether a construction route is named;
- whether a condition without a field is asserted as established or refuted;
- whether an open condition that has contributing acts, and no establishing act, is pursued through those acts or turned into a question;
- which revision of a refuted condition is taken, when its row offers several;
- how the inputs of an act are bound, when several records fit;
- the ranking of step 5.

Choosing an act that reduces the difference between the present records and the solution of the problem is an instance of means–ends analysis [newell1972]. The steps, their rules and their combination are a proposal of this method, and the sources cited supply their parts. The table of activities below is checked against the [model of the conditions](../../tests/test_gnomon/answerability_model.yml) by a [test](../../tests/test_gnomon/test_answerability_model.py), which also checks the quoted norms against the notes of the activities.

## Step 1: the questions to diagnose

The demand of the problem, its request included, is a given of the problem, and that demand fixes the [associated question](definitions/epistemic-problem.md#^associated-question) $Q_\pi$. The obstacles of the problem form a set of values of axis A, diagnosed relative to that demand, and each obstacle generates the questions listed among the [questions generated by each obstacle](definitions/problem-types.md#^obstacle-questions). The questions to diagnose are $Q_\pi$ and the generated questions, unless the presupposition of $Q_\pi$ is refuted, in which case the problem is dissolved. ^questions-to-diagnose

- **A conflict generates a choice.** A conflict can generate a question of withdrawal or a question of explanation, and the researcher chooses which to pose.
- **A negative verdict leads to a revision.** A question of verdict is answered once its verdict is obtained and accepted. When the verdict obtained for a candidate is negative, three acts follow. First, the norm of the step that produced the verdict is judged, if it is pending. Next, the verdict is accepted. Finally, the candidate is withdrawn by `belief-revision` or revised, as the researcher chooses. A revised candidate must differ from every candidate already withdrawn, so the record of withdrawn candidates prevents a return to an earlier state. When no candidate remains, the obstacle becomes an open gap. For an unwarranted claim, `belief-revision` withdraws the accepted claim from the background $K$ instead.
- **A question of explication judges by adequacy.** Its candidates are candidate criteria, judged by their adequacy to the explicandum, not by truth [carnap1950_logical]. For such a question, the conditions of discrimination and bridging are read with adequacy as the standard, and the explicandum is not a slot of the question.

## Step 2: diagnose the open conditions

The conditions of a question are diagnosed on each of its routes. The route of selection is always diagnosed. The [route of construction](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^routes) is diagnosed when a procedure is named that would yield a direct answer. ^open-condition

**Rule: work on an open condition.** A condition is *open* when its state is *not established* and every condition on which it [depends](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^condition-dependencies) is in the state *established*. A dependency on an answer to another question is met by an accepted answer or by a provisional one. A provisional answer leaves every result that rests on it conditional. A refuted condition is handled by a revision in step 3.

- **The diagnosis does not stall on the order.** Within one question, the dependencies have no cycle. Therefore, when no condition of a route is refuted, either all its conditions are established or at least one is open.
- **A condition can be asserted.** When the records hold no field for a condition, the researcher can state the condition as established or as refuted, with pointers to the records or the publications that show it. The [types of problem](definitions/problem-types.md) treat a conflict with a publication in the same way.
- **Once every condition of a route is established, the acts that obtain the answer become ready.** The acts that exercise access obtain the quantity, a combination step composes the answer, and an evaluation decides whether the answer satisfies the question. An interrogative act that obtains the answer to the question requires the soundness of that question, as the [rule on interrogative moves](definitions/conditions-of-answerability.md#^condition-dependencies) states.

## Step 3: act on each condition

### An open condition

An activity **establishes** a condition when its output supplies the object to which the condition refers and its norm, once passed, guarantees that the condition holds. An activity **contributes** to a condition when its output is a part of that object or a step toward it, or when its norm does not guarantee the condition. The table lists the activities of the [registry](../../src/gnomon/data/registries/activities.yml) by identifier, and marks as a *gap* each act that no registered activity types yet. ^condition-activities

| Condition | Activities that establish it | Activities that contribute to it | Gaps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Definiteness | `explication`, `explicit-definition`, `operationalization` | `concept-revision`, `delimitation`, `formalization` | no activity produces a first concept from an explicandum; no activity raises a phenomenon to commitment `specified` |
| Soundness | `phenomenon-detection`, for an empirical presupposition; `derivation`, for a formal presupposition | — | no activity records a negative detection |
| Answer type | — | `question-formulation`, which types the answer space; `delimitation`, which fixes the extent through the target | no activity fills or revises the fields of the epistemic task |
| Candidates | — | `abduction`, `induction`, `analogical-inference`, `causal-inference`, `decomposition-localization`, `model-analogy`, which propose candidates; `de-idealization`, `idealization`, `abstraction`, `formalization`, `fitting`, which revise a candidate; `derivation`, whose consequence enters a candidate as a law with a derived warrant; `critique`, which names objections | no activity checks the admissibility of a candidate; no activity builds a model from a target specification and a background without a phenomenon; no activity retrieves an established result as a candidate; no activity derives from a candidate together with the admitted laws, since `derivation` takes one record |
| Discrimination | `experimental-design`, when its model represents the rival answers | `derivation`, `simulation`, `prediction`, `thought-experiment`, each supplying the value of a quantity under one candidate | no activity compares the consequences of rival candidates held as separate records |
| Access | `experimental-design`, `operationalization` | — | no activity develops a proof technique or an algorithm |
| Bridging | `derivation` of a linking lemma; `background-appraisal`, which admits an auxiliary law with its warrant | `operationalization`, whose scale is not yet a validated measurement model | — |
| Scope | `testing` of a prediction for the target against its data | `robustness-analysis`, `de-idealization`, `transfer`, `validation`, `prediction` | — |

Once every condition of a route is established, the acts that obtain and accept the answer come next:

- the acts that exercise access: `measurement`, `observation`, `intervention`, `simulation`, and `derivation` along the route of construction;
- the acts that compose and evaluate the answer: `testing`, `confirmation`, `model-selection`, `inference-to-the-best-explanation`, `validation`, `critique`; `explanation`, `classification`, `unification`, `reduction` or `integration`, when the demand asks for their output type;
- the act that changes the status of the records: `belief-revision`, which takes the verdict of the evaluation.

Further gaps concern every route:

- no activity accepts a derived consequence, since `belief-revision` takes only a verdict, a credence or a ranking;
- no activity turns a derived consequence into a phenomenon;
- no activity judges the norm of a plan step;
- a question of explication has no record form, since its explicandum is not a slot.

**When no activity establishes an open condition, the condition can become a question.** The new question asks for the object that the condition lacks. The question-decomposition framework states this move as its central step: each unmet condition becomes a subquestion. The activity `question-decomposition` produces the new question from a question record. When $Q_\pi$ has no question record yet, `question-formulation` first states a question schema, and `subject-identification` raises its slots, so that the record becomes a question. A demand that is a conjunction is decomposed in the same way, with one question per conjunct, linked by `subquestion-of`. A question generated from a condition is not a part of the answer, so `subquestion-of` does not fit it, and the closed list of links between questions has no relation for it yet. ^condition-to-question

### A refuted condition

A refuted condition calls for a revision, and the researcher chooses among the revisions of its row. ^refuted-condition

| Refuted condition | Revisions |
| --- | --- |
| Soundness of $Q_\pi$ | the problem is dissolved, as the [outcomes of a problem](definitions/epistemic-problem.md#^problem-outcomes) state |
| Soundness of a generated question | the generated question is revised, or abandoned while the problem stands |
| Definiteness, when no adequate criterion exists | the term is sharpened when it is vague, replaced by a concept that separates the situations which it conflated, or abandoned when its extension is empty |
| Answer type, when no answer can meet it | the epistemic task is revised, for instance by a weaker modal strength or a coarser accuracy |
| Discrimination | the contrast or the domain of the question is narrowed; its resolution is coarsened so that the inseparable candidates form one answer; or the development of a new quantity or instrument becomes a question |
| Access | the other route is taken, or the development of a procedure becomes a question |
| Bridging | another quantity is chosen, or the missing auxiliary hypothesis becomes a question |
| Scope | the scope of the question is restricted, or the answer is de-idealized |

The condition of candidates has no row. When all candidates fail the admissibility tests or are eliminated, the condition is not established, and its acts are those of an open condition. When the question presupposed that its candidates were complete, the elimination of all of them refutes that presupposition, and the row of soundness applies.

No registered activity revises a question, narrows its domain or its contrast, or decomposes a problem into subproblems. The [question-hierarchy framework](../methods-reasoning/question-hierarchy-framework.md) names three revisions of a question: disambiguation, restriction of scope, and replacement of a concept. After any revision that changes a diagnosed question, the procedure restarts from step 1. Apart from any refutation, the main question is also re-examined at intervals, to find whether it needs one of these three revisions, as the question-hierarchy framework requires.

## Step 4: readiness of an act

An **act instance** is an activity whose inputs are bound to records, to the outputs of steps, or, for a `source`, to pointers. The condition served by the instance guides the binding. ^ready-act

- **An input must exist.** When an input does not exist, the search proceeds backward through the registry: the missing input becomes a subgoal, and the activities that produce it are examined in turn.
- **An input must be warranted.** A record bound to an input lacks warrant when a step of the plan that produced the record failed its norm, whatever the output type of that step. An act that uses it inherits the failure, so the repair of that step becomes a subgoal, ranked before the act. A record produced by a step whose verdict is pending can be used, and the result of the act stays conditional on that verdict. Readiness reads only the steps that the plan records.
- **A pending norm stays to be judged.** When the record produced by a step is stabilized while the verdict of the step is pending, the norm of the step remains to be judged, and the record is treated as the product of a pending step until then.
- **The necessity of a subgoal is read over plans.** A missing input is necessary for the goal when every plan that reaches the goal contains an act instance that requires it. A particular act that produces that input is necessary only when, in addition, no other activity produces the input. Even a necessary subgoal does not reach the goal, since the remaining steps are activities whose norms can fail. The problem reduces to the problem of producing that input only when the remaining steps form a recorded, uniform method, as the definition of [reduction](definitions/epistemic-problem.md#^reduction) requires. Hierarchical task-network planning decomposes a task by methods that replace it with subtasks [erol1994]. Erotetic search scenarios start from a principal question and lead, through auxiliary questions, to its answers [wisniewski2013].

## Step 5: ranking

Before the ranking, the question-hierarchy framework gives each act one of three statuses by its test of relevance: necessary, useful or decorative. Decorative acts are removed, and the necessary and useful acts are compared by the first five criteria below. The sixth criterion orders the open conditions of one route, not the acts. ^ranking-criteria

| Criterion | Preferred | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnostic power | the act whose outcome most constrains the admissible answers, by eliminating candidates, refuting an assumption, or forcing a reformulation of the question | question-hierarchy framework; [lindley1956]; [chaloner1995] |
| Least favorable outcome | the act whose least favorable plausible outcome eliminates the largest share of the live candidates | question-hierarchy framework; maximin, after [wald1950] |
| Structural centrality | the act that bears on a load-bearing assumption, concept or mechanism, whose failure would restructure the problem | question-hierarchy framework |
| Feasibility | the act that the available means, or a simplified setting, allow to complete now, with the higher probability of completion and at the lower cost | question-hierarchy framework; question-decomposition framework |
| Strength of warrant | the act that delivers the stronger warrant for the answer | question-decomposition framework |
| Order of tests | among the open conditions of one route, the condition whose test has the lowest ratio of cost to probability of refutation | [unluyurt2025] |

- **Decisive negative results carry the most weight.** Confirmation accumulates slowly, whereas one decisive negative result eliminates a structural hypothesis. Therefore, the criteria of diagnostic power and of the least favorable outcome favor acts designed as potential refutations of an assumption, as Popper's severe tests are [popper1963].
- **Expected information gain measures diagnostic power over candidates.** Lindley measures the information that an experiment provides by the expected reduction of the uncertainty over the parameters or hypotheses [lindley1956]. Bayesian experimental design maximizes an expected utility, and Lindley's information is one choice of that utility [chaloner1995]. The measure is defined over candidates already proposed, so it does not capture the outcome that forces a reformulation.
- **The order of tests is a heuristic.** For a series system, where every test must pass, testing in increasing ratio of cost to probability of failure minimizes the expected cost, a result due to Butterworth [unluyurt2025]. The result assumes tests with two outcomes, fixed costs, independent outcomes, a stop at the first failure, and no order imposed. The conditions of a route meet none of these assumptions exactly: an act can leave a condition not established, a refutation for lack of means is revisable, and correlated conditions such as discrimination and access are not independent. The dependencies between conditions also impose an order. Under general precedence constraints the problem of the optimal order is NP-complete, and Garey's polynomial algorithm for out-trees does not apply, since bridging depends on several conditions [unluyurt2025]. Sorting the open conditions by the ratio is therefore a heuristic, analogous to the fail-first principle of search over constraints [haralick1980].
- **Feasibility and the order of tests use different probabilities.** Feasibility uses the probability that an act completes. The order of tests uses the probability that a condition is refuted.
- **The criteria can conflict.** A feasible act can have a low diagnostic power. The question-hierarchy framework settles the main conflict by one rule: prefer the act that is both highly constraining and reasonably tractable. Beyond that rule, the researcher weighs the criteria, and the first act whose value reaches a level stated in advance can be accepted without a search for the best one, as satisficing allows [simon1956].

Four heuristics propose acts instead of ranking them:

- **Problem solving.** Look at the unknown; find a related problem already solved; solve a part of the problem; specialize or generalize; work backward [polya1945].
- **Positive heuristic.** A research programme plans a sequence of models, from an idealized model to corrected models [lakatos1970]. *Example*: a mean-field limit followed by its finite-size corrections, each step typed by `de-idealization`.
- **Discovery of mechanisms.** Schema instantiation, modular subassembly, forward chaining from the causes, and backward chaining from the effects [craver2013].
- **Strategic rules.** The interrogative model separates its definitory rules, which state the admissible moves, from its strategic rules, which state the good moves [hintikka1999]. A deductive step can establish the presupposition of a question, and thereby allow the interrogative moves that obtain its answer.

## The acts named in practice

- **Raising a placeholder.** A slot is raised to `identified` when the question is posed, since a question names each referent. A slot is raised to `specified` when an open condition needs the definiteness of its term: soundness, the answer type, or access along the route of construction. A candidate stated in the [answer form](answer-form.md) needs `specified` slots, since the answer form refers to declared symbols. A candidate stated in words only does not.
- **Refining the question.** A question is refined by the revisions of step 3: when the soundness of a generated question, the answer type, discrimination or scope is refuted, or when a concept is replaced after definiteness is refuted.
- **Specifying the form of the answer.** The answer type is one of the conditions, and the epistemic task fixes it as far as its fields are filled. A field can be left open on purpose and narrowed later by a recorded revision. The required accuracy and the proof obligations of the task fix the standard of warrant. The tests of success are drawn from the specifications of the question, as the [conditions of satisfaction](satisfaction-conditions.md) state.
- **Decomposing.** A question is decomposed when an open condition has no establishing activity and the researcher turns it into a question, or when its demand is a conjunction.
- **Deriving new claims.** A derivation establishes soundness for a formal presupposition and bridging through a linking lemma, and it contributes to discrimination through the consequences of one candidate, and to the candidates through a derived law. Along the route of construction, a derivation exercises access.

## Dependencies between acts

The dependencies between acts form cycles among types: a concept serves a question, the question selects a model, and the model can revise the concept. These cycles are resolved by iteration, under the conditions of [controlled iteration](specification-order.md#^controlled-iteration). Within the procedure, an iteration starts in three places: a refuted condition triggers a revision, a withdrawn record removes the warrant of the acts that used it, and a provisional answer to another question is later revised. The question-hierarchy framework records withdrawn records by marking each sub-question with the hypothesis on which it is conditioned.

## Example

The facts below are those of the [records of the inquiry into delayed generalization](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/_index.md). The [problem](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/problem.yml) demands the determinants of the delay between fitting and generalization, a derivation of its duration, and its variation across random initializations. The question record `quest.grokking.explain_delay` addresses the problem, and the example takes it as the record of $Q_\pi$. One [candidate answer](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/answer.yml) has status `hypothesized`: the delay results from the slow structuring of the embedding representation.

1. **Questions.** The set $H$ holds one candidate and none is accepted, which axis A records as *unassessed candidates*, whether or not an assessment exists. The questions to diagnose are $Q_\pi$ and the question of verdict on the candidate.
2. **Diagnosis.**
    - *The verdict.* The [assessment](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/assessment.yml) of the candidate is stabilized, and the assessment records `fail` for four admissibility tests (scope, grain, modal strength and product) and for three warrant tests (closure, fidelity and robustness). The test of admissible explanans is pending. In the [plan](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/plan.yml), the step `test` that produced the assessment records the verdict `pending`. The negative verdict is therefore obtained, and it is not yet accepted.
    - *$Q_\pi$.* The answer type is asserted as established as far as the task fixes it: the aim `explanation`, the relation `mathematical` and the modal strength `actual` are filled, and the admissible explanans is left open on purpose. Soundness is established, since the [phenomenon](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/phenomenon.yml) has the evidential status `established`. The condition of candidates is not established, since the only candidate fails four admissibility tests. Scope fails because the account is derived for a toy architecture, whereas the question ranges over finite overparameterized networks. Grain fails because the account does not derive the distribution of the delay across initializations. The evidence for modal strength cites the class, and the evidence for product cites both the class and the distribution. The condition of candidates is therefore open.
3. **Acts.**
    - *The verdict.* The norm of the step `test` is to be judged, an act that no registered activity types. Once the verdict is accepted, the candidate is withdrawn by `belief-revision` or revised, as the researcher chooses.
    - *The candidates of $Q_\pi$.* No activity establishes the condition, and several contribute, so the researcher chooses between pursuing the contributing acts and posing a question. The contributing acts are of two kinds. The first kind proposes a rival, such as `abduction`, which takes the phenomenon record. The second kind revises the candidate: `de-idealization` extends the candidate model from its toy architecture toward the reference class, and a derivation adds the distribution across initializations. That derivation must combine the candidate with the law of initialization, which the [background](../../test/grokking-delayed-generalization/background.yml) admits and the answer record does not state, and no registered activity derives from a candidate together with the admitted laws.
4. **Readiness.**
    - `abduction` binds the phenomenon record, produced by the step `identify` with the verdict `pass`, so it is ready. The plan records no step that raised the phenomenon to `specified`, so readiness reads only that verdict.
    - `de-idealization` binds the answer record, produced by the steps `propose` and `derive` with the verdicts `pass` and `pending`, and the target-system record, produced by the step `delimit` with the verdict `pending`. The instance is ready, and its result stays conditional on both pending verdicts.
    - The derivation of the distribution is blocked twice: its activity is a gap, and the background record was produced by the step `admit`, whose verdict is `fail`, so the repair of that step is a subgoal ranked before it.
5. **Ranking.** The order between judging the norm of `test`, revising the set of candidates, and pursuing `abduction` or `de-idealization` is left to the researcher, by the criteria of step 5. Within the revision of the candidate, the repair of the step `admit` precedes the derivation of the distribution by the rule of readiness, not by the ranking.
