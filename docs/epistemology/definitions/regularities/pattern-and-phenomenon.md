# Pattern and phenomenon

> [!QUESTION] Goal: does a statement name a regularity across instances or one structured pattern, and when does either count as a phenomenon?

"Pattern" covers two objects of different types. A **regularity** is an invariant across a set of instances: configurations, states, histories or data. A **spatial or temporal pattern** is one structured configuration or trajectory, such as stripes or an oscillation. A **phenomenon** is a feature of the world that an inquiry takes as its explanandum, detected through data. The requirements of a record on a phenomenon are stated in the [specification of the phenomenon](../../subject/phenomenon.md). This note fixes the senses of the terms.

## Senses

| Term | Community | Definition | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regularity, as a real pattern | philosophy of mind and science | a regularity is *real* when a description that uses it compresses the data, relative to a description that lists every value | [dennett1991] |
| Regularity, as causal states | computational mechanics | the structure of a process captured by its causal states, the classes of pasts that predict the same future; its size is measured by the statistical complexity | [shalizi2001] |
| Spatial or temporal pattern | physics of pattern formation | a spatial or temporal structure that arises when a uniform state of a driven system loses stability | [cross1993] |
| Phenomenon | philosophy of science | a stable, repeatable feature of the world, explained by theories and detected through data | [bogen1988]; [woodward1989] |
| Phenomenon | empiricist philosophy of science | an observable entity: an object, an event or a process | [vanfraassen2008] |
| Appearance | empiricist philosophy of science | the content of an observation, or the outcome of a measurement | [vanfraassen2008] |
| Phenomenon | realist ontology | a fact as it appears to an observer | [bunge1977] |

The three senses of phenomenon differ in their requirements. The first requires stability and leaves observability open, since a phenomenon may be detected only through instruments and statistics [bogen1988]. The second requires observability. The third requires only an observer.

## Distinctions

- **A regularity is invariant across instances, whereas a spatial or temporal pattern is one structured instance.** A [configuration](../quantities/state-and-configuration.md) is the arrangement of the constituents at one instant, and a pattern in the sense of pattern formation is one such configuration, or one trajectory, with a structure. A regularity is the feature shared by a set of instances. *Example*: the stripes formed by one run of a reaction–diffusion system are a spatial pattern. The regularity is that every run above a threshold of the drive forms stripes.
- **A regularity is a law₁ only when it satisfies a criterion of lawhood.** The [criteria of lawhood](law.md#^lawhood) include invariance under interventions, and a regularity that satisfies one of them is a law₁. An accidental regularity, such as the silver coins of one pocket, is not a law₁.
- **A phenomenon differs from its appearances.** In the empiricist sense, a phenomenon is an observable entity, and an appearance is the content of an observation of that entity, or the outcome of a measurement of it [vanfraassen2008]. One phenomenon has many appearances, one per observation or per measurement.
- **Data are not phenomena.** Data are records specific to one procedure, whereas a phenomenon is the feature detectable by several procedures [bogen1988] [woodward1989]. Data record appearances, in the empiricist reading. *Example*: the recorded voltage traces of one session are data, and the gamma oscillation inferred from sessions recorded across laboratories is a phenomenon.
- **A regularity or a pattern becomes a phenomenon when it is selected as explanandum, and a regularity must also be established as stable.** Selection and stability are two separate conditions. When a regularity is found by exploration and not yet tested for stability, it is a candidate phenomenon. However, only a phenomenon of the category *regularity* must be stable. The [specification of the phenomenon](../../subject/phenomenon.md) also admits particular occurrences, capacities and structural properties as explananda. A phenomenon can also occur only once [craver2024].

## Consequences for a record

- **The evidential status of a phenomenon records how far its stability is established.** The [specification of the phenomenon](../../subject/phenomenon.md) grades the status from *established* to *derived within a model*, and the detection criterion states the test required of a candidate regularity.
- **A phenomenon of an exploratory inquiry is a regularity not yet selected.** The [exploratory question](../../subject/phenomenon.md#^exploratory-phenomenon) leaves the phenomenon open and fixes the search space in which regularities are sought.
- **A spatial or temporal pattern enters a record as one occurrence or through an observable.** One pattern, such as the oscillation of one run, is a phenomenon of category *particular-occurrence*. The features of the pattern, such as its frequency, are observables over a [declared window](../change/process-event-history.md#^window-observable).
