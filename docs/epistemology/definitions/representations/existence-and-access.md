# Existence and access

> [!QUESTION] Goal: what does an inquiry presuppose about the existence of the objects that it studies and about its access to them, and which positions of the philosophy of science answer each question?

An object of study is reached through a description of its constituents, its properties, its laws and the procedures that measure it or act on it. Two questions about that description are often merged, although the positions of the philosophy of science answer them separately. ^two-questions

- The **question of existence** asks whether the object and its features exist independently of any description of them.
- The **question of access** asks whether the object can be known, or referred to, other than through a description.

## Mediated access

Most current positions answer the question of access in the same way: an object is known only through a description, and the description is itself a representation. Three results of the philosophy of science support that answer. ^mediated-access

- **Phenomena are inferred from data.** Data are records of one procedure, whereas a phenomenon is a stable feature of the world inferred from many such records [bogen1988].
- **A measurement outcome is inferred through a model.** A model of the instrument and of its interaction with the object fixes which quantity a reading measures [tal2013].
- **A target is specified by the modeller.** A target system is picked out of the world by choices of boundary, of grain and of the features retained, and the specification of a target is a step of modelling on its own [weisberg2013] [elliottgraves2020].

A description can nevertheless fix its object without describing it correctly. A definite description fixes the referent of a term, and the referent stays fixed while later descriptions revise the properties ascribed to it [kripke1980]. Therefore, a mediated access is compatible with an object that the inquiry has misdescribed.

## Positions on existence and on success

Scientific realism combines three commitments: a metaphysical commitment to a world that exists independently of minds, a semantic commitment to a literal reading of scientific claims, and an epistemological commitment by which those claims, read literally, constitute knowledge [chakravartty2017]. The other positions reject or weaken one of these commitments. ^realism-commitments

| Position | Existence of the object | Achievement of a successful description | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific realism | the world investigated exists independently of minds | approximate truth, read literally, including about unobservable entities; the success of science would otherwise be a miracle | [chakravartty2017] |
| Epistemic structural realism | the world exists independently, and its individuals stay unknown | truth about the structure of the world, which survives a change of theory, and no knowledge of the nature of its relata | [ladyman2023] |
| Ontic structural realism | structure is ontologically primary, and individuals are not | truth about structure, which is all there is to know | [ladyman2023] |
| Constructive empiricism | agnostic about the world beyond the observable | empirical adequacy: a theory is accepted with the belief only that the observable phenomena embed in one of its models | [mohler2025] [vanfraassen1980] |
| Empiricist structuralism | as in constructive empiricism | a representation of phenomena as embeddable in abstract models; phenomena and models remain distinct domains | [vanfraassen2008] |
| Instrumentalism | not a question that science answers | success in predicting observable phenomena; a description of unobservables is an instrument, without a literal meaning | [chakravartty2017] |
| Internal realism | objects exist relative to a conceptual scheme | truth as rational acceptability in an ideal limit, never correspondence with an unconceptualized reality | [putnam1981] |
| Perspectival realism | the world exists, and knowledge of it is situated in scientific perspectives | knowledge of modally robust phenomena, which hold across experimental, theoretical and modelling perspectives; models serve as inferential blueprints | [massimi2022] |

The positions differ on existence and on the achievement of a successful description. The [mediated access](#^mediated-access) stated above is compatible with each of them.

## Commitments of the method

The method of `gnomon` is committed on access and on the semantics of claims, and neutral on existence. The decision on the [neutrality of the records](../../../adr/neutral-records-on-existence.md) fixes that stance for the records. ^method-stance

- **Every content of a record is a representation.** A record holds constructs only, as the [statuses of a term](../systems/things-and-constructs.md#^layer-test) place them. The target specification is a construct that delimits the target, and an error in it is corrected by revising the record.
- **The description of a target is a model of that target.** A target specification with its background represents the target, so it is a model in the [representational sense](model.md#^target-description-is-a-model). The method reserves the model record for a system other than the target, studied in its place.
- **A claim is read literally, and it can be false.** A law statement, a bridge law or a binding asserts a relation that can fail of the target, whereas a declaration makes no claim and is assessed only for its adequacy. The method therefore shares the semantic commitment of realism, and it excludes instrumentalism, which denies a literal meaning to claims about unobservables.
- **No record states whether its object exists independently.** A realist reads a claim as true or false of a world that exists independently. An empiricist reads the same claim as accepted for its empirical adequacy. Both readings use the same records, the same checks and the same tests of warrant, since each test compares a claim with recorded data or with admitted laws.
- **Several models of one target can adopt several perspectives.** The [perspective of a model](../../answer/model-and-binding.md#^perspective) is its choice of bearers. Several perspectives on one system are admitted together, as perspectival realism describes, without making the records perspectival in their stance on existence.

## Distinctions

- **A neutral stance on existence differs from instrumentalism.** Instrumentalism denies that a claim about unobservables is true or false. The method keeps that claim true or false, and it declines only to state whether accepting the claim commits to the existence of the objects that it describes.
- **Two steps of representation answer two needs.** The description of the target fixes the vocabulary and the admitted laws of the inquiry. A model system is a second object, studied because it is simpler, better known or computable, and a [binding](../../answer/model-and-binding.md#^map-versus-binding) carries its results to the target. Both are representations: the distinction separates a description of the target from a description of a stand-in, never a representation from reality.
- **Adequacy and truth apply to different contents.** A declaration of a quantity is adequate or inadequate, as a vocabulary is. A claim stated in that vocabulary is true or false of the target in a realist reading, and empirically adequate or not in an empiricist reading.
