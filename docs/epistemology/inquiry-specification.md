#  Specifying an inquiry

> [!QUESTION] Goal: what must be fixed to specify a research objective?

> [!DANGER] No single canonical decomposition across philosophy of science: several traditions are combined in this synthesis.

## Levels of specification

The philosophical literature supports separating **distinct logical roles** of a research objective:

| Level of specification                                                   | Question                                                                   | Typical concepts                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. [Question](question-specification.md)**                             | What objects is the inquiry about?                                         | target system $S$, phenomenon/explanandum $P$, contrast class $X$, scope/granularity $\sigma$                                                                                                              |
| **2. [Epistemic task](epistemic-task.md)**                               | What *kind* of knowledge or *achievement* is sought?                       | epistemic aim $A$ (description vs prediction vs explanation vs understanding); relevance relations $R$ (causal vs mechanistic vs mathematical relation); modal status $\mu$ (how-actually vs how-possibly) |
| **3. [Epistemic and pragmatic virtues](epistemic-pragmatic-virtues.md)** | What must the answer accomplish to be practically or cognitively *useful*? | unification, economy, pragmatic use, robustness, intelligibility / qualitative grasp, tractability, etc                                                                                                    |
| **4. Answer / model form**                                               | What is the form of the proposed answer?                                   | explanans $E$, variables $V$, mechanisms $M$, equations, organization, idealizations $I$, etc                                                                                                              |

> [!CHECK] Support from the literature
> - **Object (1) versus Task (2)**. The same question can admit multiple types of answers. Van Fraassen already distinguishes the question being asked (the *explanandum* and *contrast class*) from the sort of information the question requests (the context-dependent *relevance relation*). More broadly, philosophy of science distinguishes epistemic achievements such as explanation and understanding from their objects.
> - **Task (2) versus Success conditions (3)** Parker's adequacy-for-purpose framework requires first fixing the purpose and what would constitute achieving it, then assessing whether the model can reliably achieve that purpose. Van Fraassen's likewise separates specification of the explanatory question from the rules that evaluate candidate answers.
> - **Explanation versus Understanding (2,3)**. Contemporary epistemology explicitly treats understanding as a further epistemic achievement, often characterized by _grasping_ explanatory, structural, or modal relations rather than merely possessing a correct explanation. Kvanvig emphasizes grasp of explanatory/coherence relations; Grimm emphasizes grasp of modal dependencies.

## Answer / model forms

The previous specifications and requirements finally constrain the **proposed answer, representation or model**.

A particular explanatory model can be described:
$$M=\langle E,V,L,O,I,\ldots\rangle $$
where $E$ may contain explanatory factors ("explanans"), $V$ variables, $L$ laws or structural relations, $O$ organization, and $I$ idealizations.

> [!WARNING]
> This layer should be distinguished from the **objects of the inquiry**. In particular, the *explanans* $E$ (Hempel–Oppenheim) is generally *not part of the initial research objective*, alongside $S$, $P$, and $X$. Usually $S$, $P$, and perhaps $X$ are *fixed by the question*, whereas $E$ is precisely what inquiry is attempting to discover (i.e. the sought answer).

*Example*: 

- target system $S$: a neuronal network,
- explanandum $P$: the emergence of synchronous oscillations in that network under condition $C$,
- contrast class $X$: asynchronous activity under the same condition $C$,
- explanans $E$: the network's connectivity pattern and synaptic dynamics that produce the oscillatory behavior.
