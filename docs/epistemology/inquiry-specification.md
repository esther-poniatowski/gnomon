#  Specifying an inquiry

> [!QUESTION] Goal: what must be fixed to specify a research objective?

> [!DANGER] No single canonical decomposition across philosophy of science: several traditions are combined in this synthesis.

## Levels of specification

The philosophical literature supports separating **distinct logical roles** of a research objective:

| Level of specification                                                   | Question                                                                   | Typical concepts                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. [Subject of inquiry](subject-of-inquiry.md)**                       | What objects is the inquiry about?                                         | target system $S$, phenomenon/explanandum $P$, contrast class $X$, domain of conditions $D$                                                                                                                |
| **2. [Epistemic task](epistemic-task.md)**                               | What *kind* of knowledge or *achievement* is sought?                       | epistemic aim $A$ (description vs prediction vs explanation vs understanding); relevance relations $R$ (causal vs mechanistic vs mathematical relation); modal status $\mu$ (how-actually vs how-possibly); level of the explanans $\lambda$; required accuracy $\tau$ |
| **3. [Epistemic and pragmatic virtues](epistemic-pragmatic-virtues.md)** | What must the answer accomplish to be practically or cognitively *useful*? | unification, economy, pragmatic use, robustness, intelligibility / qualitative grasp, tractability, etc                                                                                                    |
| **4. [Answer form](answer-form.md) and [conditions of satisfaction](satisfaction-conditions.md)** | What form must the answer take, and which tests must it pass?      | constituents $C$, terms $V$, laws or structural relations $L$, organization $O$, idealizations $I$, explanans $E$; admissibility, warrant and appraisal                                                                     |

Each level names its components with the letters of the [notation registry](notation.md), where one letter keeps one meaning across the notes.

**Subject (1) versus Task (2)**. A research question combines the [subject of inquiry](subject-of-inquiry.md) $\Sigma$ with an [epistemic task](epistemic-task.md) $T$: 
$$Q=\langle \Sigma,T\rangle$$
Indeed, the same question can admit multiple types of answers. In particular, the epistemic aim selects the interrogative word.

*Example*: one subject yields different questions under different epistemic aims:

- explanation: "In system $S$ under conditions $D$, why/how does $P$ occur rather than $X$?"
- description: "In system $S$ under conditions $D$, what features does $P$ have?"
- prediction: "In system $S$ under conditions $D$, does $P$ occur when a given condition changes?"

Support from the literature:

- In erotetic logic, a question divides into a *subject* and a *request*: the subject presents alternatives, and the request specifies what selection among them the answer must make (Belnap and Steel, [belnap1976]). 
- Van Fraassen distinguishes the question being asked (the *explanandum* and *contrast class*) from the sort of information the question requests (the context-dependent *relevance relation*). 
- More broadly, philosophy of science distinguishes epistemic achievements such as explanation and understanding from their objects.

**Task (2) versus Answer form and conditions of satisfaction (4)**. Support from the literature:

- Parker's adequacy-for-purpose framework requires first fixing the purpose and what would constitute achieving it, then assessing whether the model can reliably achieve that purpose. 
- Van Fraassen's separates specification of the explanatory question from the rules that evaluate candidate answers.

## Goals of the specification

The specification method serves four goals. ^specification-goals

| Goal                           | What the specification achieves                                                                                                                              | Support                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Transparency**               | The inquiry commits to explicit objectives, so that its claims can be checked and its analyses reproduced.                                                   | stating predictions before the data separates tests from post hoc accounts [nosek2018]                                                 |
| **Operationalization**         | Specifying the system invites a partial formalization: a repertoire of variables and relations for further analyses.                                         | formalizing forces the specification of intuitions that otherwise stay unexamined [guest2021]                                          |
| **Success conditions**         | The specification constrains which candidate answers are admissible and which are adequate: analyzing the problem determines its [conditions of satisfaction](satisfaction-conditions.md#^conditions-satisfaction). | a question is known once its possible answers are known [hamblin1958]; a model is evaluated by its adequacy for a purpose [parker2020] |
| **Guidance towards an answer** | The analysis points to the research actions that naturally answer the question and satisfy the other goals.                                                  | a question can be answered through the auxiliary questions it generates [wisniewski2013]                                               |
