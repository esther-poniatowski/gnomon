# Consequences, impasses and understanding

> [!QUESTION] Goal: what does a derivation establish, and what does it teach beyond the statements established?

A derivation ends in consequences, in an impasse, or in a refutation, as the [outcomes of a derivation](derivation.md) state. Each outcome traces to its premises. Beyond its outcomes, a derivation teaches why they hold and where else the same reasoning applies, at three grains.

## Consequence

A **consequence** is a statement established by a derivation, and it answers a [sought](sought.md). A consequence answering the root sought is recorded at one of the places where the method records consequences, as the [records of a consequence](../../processes/activities/model-analysis.md#^consequence-records) state:

- a law marked as derived by a model or a background;
- the block of model properties of an answer;
- a settled proof obligation.

The derivation names that record, and does not restate the statement.

A **lemma** is the consequence of a subordinate sought. A lemma stays in the derivation as an intermediate object, as the [criterion of the boundary](../../processes/units-of-work.md#^work-unit-criterion) requires. When a lemma holds of a model or a background beyond the derivation, the researcher states it as a derived law there, and the lemma becomes a consequence recorded in that law. ^lemma

A consequence rests on the moves reaching it, and through them on the premises of the derivation. Each premise left undischarged by the derivation must appear among the grounds of the answer citing the consequence, or among the assumptions of the law stating it. A settled proof obligation belongs to the assessment of an answer, so the grounds of that answer carry its undischarged premises. Thus, the consequence carries every condition of its validity. ^consequence-premises

A consequence stays exposed to revision through its posits and its warrants, as the [exposure of a derivation](../knowledge/grounds-and-dependence.md#^derivation-exposure) states. The rule reports the exposed premises, and assigns no degree of support to the consequence.

## Impasse

An **impasse** leaves a sought without answer. The impasse names the obstacle left by the derivation, and that obstacle is a [diagnostic interpretation](interpretation.md) of the calculation. An impasse may generate a subordinate sought, or a serving question of the inquiry. A failed derivation is one of the usual triggers of a [local revision](../../processes/inquiry/question-decomposition.md#^local-revision) of the decomposition of a question. Even when a derivation ends in an impasse, the derivation is recorded, since it traces the obstacle to its premises.

## Refutation

A **refutation** shows that a statement fails. A refutation takes one of two forms: ^refutation

- a **counterexample**: a consequence of type object that falsifies a statement;
- a **refuting test** of a [posit](posit.md): a test whose result contradicts the posit.

A refutation removes the support of its target, and of every consequence resting on that target.

A counterexample bears on a proof in two respects [lakatos1976]. A **global** counterexample falsifies the conjecture, and a **local** counterexample falsifies a lemma of its proof. The two respects cross, in three cases:

- **local and global**: the counterexample falsifies a lemma and the conjecture. The conjecture may survive with the lemma incorporated as a condition, as the [replacement of a sought](sought.md#^sought-replacement) states;
- **local, not global**: the conjecture still holds for the counterexample, and only the proof needs repair;
- **global, not local**: no stated lemma fails, so the proof rests on a hidden lemma. Stating that lemma makes the counterexample local and global.

In the terms of the [kinds of defeat](../knowledge/grounds-and-dependence.md#^defeat), a global counterexample rebuts the conjecture, and a local counterexample undermines a premise of its proof.

## Understanding at three grains

A derivation records its [knowledge beyond validity](derivation.md#^beyond-validity) at three grains:

- **Gain of a move.** The [orientation](orientation.md) of a move states the gain expected from the move, before the move. Once the derivation is complete, the gain actually brought by the move is stated with the consequence. Pólya ends the solution of a problem by *looking back* at the result and at the argument [polya1945]. The obtained gain belongs to that stage.
- **Principle of a consequence.** The principle names the property that makes the consequence hold. The principle is a variation [interpretation](interpretation.md) of the composite: when the property varies within a declared family, the consequence changes accordingly. An explanatory proof rests on such a characterizing property [steiner1978] [mancosu2023]. An answer may state the determinant of an explanation only in words, as a [factor found by the answer](../answer/answer-form.md#^factor-may-be-found). The principle can supply that determinant.
- **Pattern of a derivation.** The pattern of a derivation is the [scheme](segment-and-scheme.md) applied by its segments, with the other problems within reach of that scheme. In explanation by unification, an argument pattern is a schematic argument with instructions for filling it and a classification of its inferences, and few patterns derive many phenomena [kitcher1981] [kitcher1989]. A scheme of the method has that form, so the pattern records the derivation as one instance of the scheme. Pólya asks the same question while looking back: "Can you use the result, or the method, for some other problem?" [polya1945].
