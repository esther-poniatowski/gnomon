# Theory

> [!QUESTION] Goal: which view of theories does a statement presuppose, and how does each view relate a theory to its models?

A **theory** is a body of general claims about a domain of phenomena, from which particular claims and models are obtained. Three views assign a theory to different types of object [winther2020].

## Senses

| View | A theory is | Relation to its models | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Syntactic | a set of sentences closed under deduction, whose theoretical terms are linked to observation by correspondence rules | the models are the interpretations that satisfy the sentences | [suppe1977]; [winther2020] |
| Semantic | a class of models, together with a theoretical hypothesis that some target belongs to the class or resembles one of its members | the models constitute the theory | [suppes1960]; [vanfraassen1980]; [giere1988] |
| Structuralist | a theory-element $\langle\mathsf{K},\mathsf{I}\rangle$: a core $\mathsf{K}$ and a set $\mathsf{I}$ of intended applications | the core fixes classes of models; the applications are the systems to which the theory is meant to apply | [balzer1987] |

The structuralist core lists five classes [balzer1987]:
$$\mathsf{K}=\langle\mathsf{M}_p,\mathsf{M},\mathsf{M}_{pp},\mathsf{GC},\mathsf{GL}\rangle$$

- $\mathsf{M}_p$: the potential models, the structures of the right type;
- $\mathsf{M}$: the actual models, the potential models that satisfy the laws;
- $\mathsf{M}_{pp}$: the partial potential models, the potential models stripped of the terms theoretical relative to the theory;
- $\mathsf{GC}$: the global constraints, linking the values of a quantity across models;
- $\mathsf{GL}$: the global links to other theories.

The intended applications form a subset $\mathsf{I}\subseteq\mathsf{M}_{pp}$, described without the theoretical terms. The **empirical claim** of the theory-element states that these applications can be completed into actual models that respect the constraints and links. In symbols, $\mathsf{I}\in\mathrm{Cn}(\mathsf{K})$. The empirical content $\mathrm{Cn}(\mathsf{K})$ of the core restricts $\mathrm{Pot}(\mathsf{M})\cap\mathsf{GC}\cap\mathsf{GL}$ to partial potential models, where $\mathrm{Pot}(\mathsf{M})$ is the set of subsets of $\mathsf{M}$.

## Distinctions

- **The relation between a model and a theory depends on the view.** Under the syntactic view, a model satisfies the theory and lies outside the theory. Under the semantic and structuralist views, the models make up the theory, and a theory differs from one model as a class differs from a member.
- **A theory makes an empirical claim only through a hypothesis or an application.** A class of models asserts nothing about the world. The theoretical hypothesis of the semantic view, and the empirical claim of the structuralist view, are the [claims](claim-and-hypothesis.md) that connect the class to a target.
- **A theory and a framework differ in generality.** The criterion is stated in the [vocabulary of frameworks](framework.md).

## Relations to the method

- **A target specification fixes a family of models, as a theory in the semantic view does.** The [reference class of a target specification](../../objects/subject/target-system.md) is generated by its declarations, and each member instantiates the schema with its own parameter values.
- **The structuralist distinction between theoretical and non-theoretical terms has an analogue in a record.** By an analogy proposed by this method, the quantities added by a [candidate answer](../../objects/answer/answer-form.md) to the target specification are theoretical relative to the answer. The quantities declared by the target specification play the part of $\mathsf{M}_{pp}$.
