> [!QUESTION] Goal: What principles from Philosophy of Science and Epistemology guide the construction of classifications and taxonomies?

The question of what constitutes a principled classification has been addressed by several traditions in philosophy of science and epistemology, though no unified theory gives a universal taxonomy-building algorithm. The strongest result is pluralistic, the demand is for explicit warrant.

The relevant literature can be organized along three axes:

- the **ontological status** of the classes, i.e. what kinds of entities populate the taxonomy, 
- the **epistemic criteria** that justify the partition,
- the **pragmatic constraints** imposed by the inferential or explanatory goals of the classifier, ranging across logical, explanatory, inductive, historical, and institutional functions, each demanding distinct criteria of adequacy.

The synthesis proceeds by traversing the major traditions and extracting from each the operational principle it contributes. A final consolidation states the principles that survive cross-tradition triangulation.

- [Construction principles — variant C](taxonomic-construction-principles-c.md): principles consolidated from those traditions, from declaring the function of a taxonomy to tolerating plural classifications of one domain.
- [Construction principles — variant G](taxonomic-construction-principles-g.md): principles for classifying a body of literature, each tied to the philosophical source that supports it.
- [Qualitative feature-system construction procedure](construction-procedure.md): constructs and reduces a finite crisp object–attribute description while keeping every guarantee relative to its representation and analyzed table.
- [Extension from a feature system to a taxonomy](taxonomy-extension-plan.md): separates descriptive features from the taxonomic layer, then compares four routes for constructing that layer.
- [Construction procedure revision](construction-procedure-revision.md): records the active correction phase, governing decisions and review dependencies.
