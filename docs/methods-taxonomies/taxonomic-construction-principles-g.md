# Principles for taxonomic construction - G
## Practical synthesis

A strong classification for literature synthesis should satisfy the following principles.

|Principle|Meaning|Philosophical source|
|---|---|---|
|**Declared purpose**|State whether the taxonomy serves explanation, comparison, retrieval, critique, prediction, or theory-building|Pragmatism, pluralism|
|**Explicit unit of classification**|Classify papers, concepts, methods, assumptions, models, phenomena, or evidential roles, not all at once|Logical classification|
|**Stable basis per level**|Each level should use one discriminator unless cross-classification is intended|Aristotle, Kant, Apostel|
|**Inferential fruitfulness**|Categories should support non-trivial generalizations|Goodman, Quine, natural kinds|
|**Explanatory grounding**|Prefer categories tied to mechanisms, structures, or stable property clusters|Boyd, natural-kind theory|
|**Conceptual exactness**|Replace vague terms by sharpened definitions while preserving relevant usage|Carnap, Hempel|
|**Plural taxonomies when needed**|Use multiple coordinated classifications rather than one overloaded tree|Dupré, Chang|
|**Historical fidelity**|Preserve paradigm-specific vocabularies before translating them|Kuhn|
|**Tolerance for family resemblance**|Use polythetic categories when no essence exists|Wittgenstein|
|**Reflexive audit**|Examine what the taxonomy hides, privileges, or institutionalizes|Hacking, Bowker, Star|

The most defensible strategy is therefore not to eliminate arbitrariness, but to **discipline it**. A classification becomes rigorous when each classificatory decision is tied to an explicit epistemic function, tested against examples and counterexamples, and revised when it fails to support comparison, explanation, or conceptual clarification.

## 1. Logical and formal theories of classification

Classical logical approaches treat classification as a disciplined division of a domain. The central constraints are:

- **Exhaustiveness**: every relevant object must fall somewhere
    
- **Mutual exclusiveness**: categories at the same level should not overlap, unless overlap is explicitly allowed
    
- **Unity of basis**: a single level should not mix heterogeneous criteria
    
- **Non-arbitrariness of division**: divisions should cut along meaningful articulations, not merely convenient oppositions
    
- **Avoidance of purely negative classes**: categories such as “non-X” often fail to define a positive type
    

These ideas appear from Plato and Aristotle through Kant and later formal theories of classification. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy notes that Kant’s rules for logical division require mutually exclusive members whose union restores the divided concept, and later discusses Apostel’s stricter criteria: every level needs a basis of division, no new basis should be introduced before the previous one is exhausted, divisions should be essential, and negative defining properties should be avoided. ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/classification-in-science/ "Classification | Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy"))

**Use for literature synthesis:** this approach is valuable when the researcher needs a clean taxonomy, for example:

```text
methods
├── mechanistic methods
├── statistical-associational methods
└── phenomenological-descriptive methods
```

The risk is excessive rigidity. Many research domains contain overlapping traditions, hybrid methods, and concepts whose boundaries are not clean partitions.

## 2. Carnapian explication and logical empiricist concept formation

Carnap’s theory of **explication** is especially relevant for vocabulary clarification. The task is not merely to report how a term is used, but to replace an unclear concept by a more exact and more useful concept. Carnap’s classical criteria are:

- **similarity** to the original concept
    
- **exactness**
    
- **fruitfulness**
    
- **simplicity**
    

The explicandum must first be clarified through examples, counterexamples, and contextual restrictions; only then can a sharper explicatum be proposed. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2023/entries/carnap/methodology.html "Rudolf Carnap > D. Methodology (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy/Fall 2023 Edition)")) Hempel also treated scientific methodology as involving explication of vague concepts under criteria of adequacy. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hempel/ "Carl Hempel (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)"))

**Use for literature synthesis:** this is probably the most directly applicable framework. For each contested term, the researcher can distinguish:

```text
term in the literature
→ observed uses
→ ambiguities
→ proposed sharpened definition
→ retained uses
→ excluded uses
→ rationale for the restriction
```

This does not eliminate arbitrariness, but makes it rationally accountable.

## 3. Goodman, Quine, and the problem of projectible predicates

Goodman’s “new riddle of induction” shows that not every logically definable predicate is scientifically useful. A classification can be formally valid yet epistemically poor if its categories do not support stable induction. Goodman’s notion of **projectibility** asks whether a predicate can support legitimate generalization; his account emphasizes the role of entrenched predicates in successful past projections. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goodman/ "Nelson Goodman (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)"))

Quine connects natural kinds with similarity and induction: classifications matter because they determine which observed cases can support generalizations about unobserved cases. However, Quine also stresses that scientific maturity often replaces vague similarity with domain-specific relations, such as phylogenetic proximity in biology. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/natural-kinds/ "Natural Kinds (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)"))

**Use for literature synthesis:** a good category should not merely collect similar papers. It should support useful inferences, for example:

```text
Studies in class A tend to share assumptions, methods, validity conditions, and failure modes.
```

A poor category merely groups papers by superficial vocabulary.

## 4. Natural kinds, causal grounding, and stable property clusters

The natural-kind tradition asks whether scientific categories track real structures in the world. Contemporary philosophy of science has largely moved beyond strict essentialism. Boyd’s homeostatic property cluster account allows kinds whose members share a stable cluster of properties without any single necessary and sufficient property. Such clusters are sustained by mechanisms and can support induction and explanation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/natural-kinds/ "Natural Kinds (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)"))

Recent “epistemology-first” approaches emphasize that classifications matter because they enable successful inquiry, explanation, and induction in actual scientific practice. Slater, Magnus, Ereshefsky, Reydon, Kendig, and others are associated with this shift from metaphysical purity toward classificatory practice. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/natural-kinds/ "Natural Kinds (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)"))

**Use for literature synthesis:** when classifying studies, the relevant question becomes:

```text
Does this class correspond to a stable explanatory structure, mechanism, inferential role, or research practice?
```

This is stronger than asking whether the class has a neat verbal definition.

## 5. Pluralism and pragmatic realism

Dupré’s “promiscuous realism” is important for resisting the idea that a single taxonomy must be uniquely correct. According to this view, the world can be classified in many cross-cutting but still objectively grounded ways, depending on the theoretical interests and research questions pursued. The SEP discussion explicitly notes that different systems of classification may be equally legitimate scientifically and metaphysically. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/natural-kinds/ "Natural Kinds (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)"))

Chang’s notion of **epistemic iteration** is also relevant: taxonomy can begin from imperfect classifications and improve through successive correction. In domains with evolving taxonomies, it may be rational to maintain one official system for coordination while allowing multiple research systems until a superior alternative emerges.

**Use for literature synthesis:** the researcher should not necessarily seek one final taxonomy. A better structure may contain several coordinated classifications:

```text
Classification by object studied
Classification by explanatory aim
Classification by method
Classification by theoretical commitment
Classification by level of analysis
```

This is not a defect if each classification has a stated function.

## 6. Kuhn and taxonomic incommensurability

Kuhn’s later work connects scientific change with changes in taxonomic structure. Incommensurability is not merely disagreement over claims; it can involve different lexical and classificatory systems across paradigms or neighboring subdisciplines. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2016/entries/incommensurability/ "The Incommensurability of Scientific Theories (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy/Winter 2016 Edition)"))

**Use for literature synthesis:** when different studies use the same term differently, or different terms for structurally similar concepts, the issue may not be terminological noise. It may indicate distinct research paradigms. The framework should therefore include a layer for:

```text
term used
conceptual role
background assumptions
neighboring contrasts
implicit ontology
```

This avoids forcing premature equivalence across literatures.

## 7. Wittgensteinian family resemblance and polythetic classification

Wittgenstein’s later philosophy rejects the assumption that every meaningful category must have one common essence. Some concepts are unified by overlapping similarities rather than necessary and sufficient conditions. The SEP notes that Wittgenstein replaces the search for strict form or essence with similarity of a family-resemblance kind. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Ludwig Wittgenstein - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy"))

**Use for literature synthesis:** some research families should be classified polythetically:

```text
A study belongs to this family if it shares enough of the following traits:
- similar problem formulation
- similar explanatory aim
- similar mathematical formalism
- similar empirical protocol
- similar notion of evidence
```

This is appropriate for traditions, paradigms, and methodological families. It is less appropriate when a strict operational definition is required.

## 8. Hacking, Bowker, Star, and the consequences of classification

Hacking’s work on looping effects shows that classifications can affect the entities classified, especially in human sciences. His framework emphasizes interactions among classifications, people, institutions, knowledge, and experts. ([The British Academy](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/2043/pba151p285.pdf "10 Hacking 1573")) Bowker and Star similarly analyze classification systems as infrastructures with practical and institutional consequences; classification systems are not neutral lists but shape action, visibility, and coordination. ([Museum of Data](https://museumofdata.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Bowker_and_Star_-_Sorting_Things_Out_Intro.pdf "Sorting Things Out: introduction - Introduction: To Classify Is Human"))

**Use for literature synthesis:** this matters when a taxonomy will guide research agendas, evaluation, funding, institutional labels, or disciplinary boundaries. The researcher should ask:

```text
Which distinctions become visible?
Which distinctions disappear?
Which research programs become central or marginal?
Which terms acquire normative authority?
```
