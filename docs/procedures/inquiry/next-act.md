# Choosing the next act

> [!QUESTION] Goal: once a problem is stated, how is the next act of research found from the records, and which registered activities perform it?

The criteria by which an act is chosen are stated in the theory of the [choice of the next act](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md). This procedure applies them to the records in five steps:

1. **Questions.** List the questions to diagnose: the associated question of the problem, the questions that its obstacles generate, and the questions on which a condition depends across questions.
2. **Diagnosis.** State each [condition for obtaining an answer](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/conditions-of-answerability.md) of each question in one of its three states, `established`, `not established` or `refuted`, and find the [open conditions](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^open-condition).
3. **Acts.** Map each open condition to the registered activities that act on it, or turn it into a new question. Map each refuted condition to a [revision](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^refuted-condition). Once every condition of a route is in the state `established`, the acts that obtain the answer come next, followed by the [evaluation](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/conditions-of-answerability.md#^evaluation) of the answer.
4. **Readiness.** Keep the [ready act instances](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^ready-act): those whose inputs exist and are warranted.
5. **Ranking.** Choose among the acts kept, by the [ranking criteria](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^ranking-criteria).

Each step either returns a determinate output or presents alternatives, between which the researcher chooses, as the list of [choices left to the researcher](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^researcher-choices) states. The activity map of step 3 is checked against the [model of the conditions](../../../tests/test_gnomon/answerability_model.yml) by a [test](../../../tests/test_gnomon/test_answerability_model.py), which also checks each quoted norm against the note of its activity.

## Step 1: the questions to diagnose

The demand of the problem, its request included, is a given of the problem, and that demand fixes the [associated question](../../epistemology/objects/problem/epistemic-problem.md#^associated-question) $Q_\pi$. The obstacles of the problem form a set of values of axis A, diagnosed relative to that demand, and each obstacle generates the questions listed among the [questions generated by each obstacle](../../epistemology/objects/problem/problem-types.md#^obstacle-questions). The questions to diagnose are $Q_\pi$, the generated questions, and the questions on which a diagnosed condition depends across questions, unless a presupposition of $Q_\pi$ is refuted outright, in which case the problem is dissolved. ^questions-to-diagnose

- **A conflict generates a choice.** A conflict can generate a question of withdrawal or a question of explanation, and the researcher chooses which to pose.
- **A negative verdict leads to a revision.** A question of verdict is answered once its verdict is obtained and accepted, and the acceptance of a verdict rests on the judgment of the norm of the step that produced it. When the verdict obtained for a candidate is negative, three acts follow in order. First, the norm of the producing step is judged, if it is pending. Next, the researcher accepts the verdict. Finally, the candidate is withdrawn by `belief-revision`, or revised, as the researcher chooses. A withdrawn candidate, or a revision identical to it, returns only with a recorded new ground, such as an overturned verdict or new evidence, so the record of withdrawn candidates keeps the ground of each withdrawal. When no candidate remains, the obstacle becomes an open gap, unless the question presupposed that its candidates were complete, in which case its soundness is refuted.
- **An unwarranted claim stays admitted with its warrant.** A claim admitted with a warrant weaker than the demand requires, such as a law with the warrant `fitted`, stays admitted with that warrant and serves as a premise of other work, each result resting on it staying conditional on it. This pragmatic acceptance adopts the claim as a premise without claiming more for it [cohen1992]. The claim with the warrant that the demand requires, such as a law derived from the admitted laws, becomes the candidate of the question of verdict. A claim without any support enters the background record only by a recorded decision of the researcher, as a working assumption under the warrant `posited`: it is then admitted, not accepted. Such a decision establishes no fact, so its result is a revisable assumption, as the [terminal acts](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/question-decomposition.md#^terminal-acts) state.
- **A question of explication judges by the adequacy of an explication.** Its candidates are candidate criteria, judged by their adequacy to the explicandum, not by truth [carnap1950_logical]. For such a question, the conditions of discrimination and bridging are read with that adequacy as the standard, and the explicandum is not a slot of the question.

## Step 2: diagnose the open conditions

The conditions of a question are diagnosed on each of its routes. The route of selection is always diagnosed. The [route of construction](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/conditions-of-answerability.md#^routes) is diagnosed when a procedure is named that would yield a direct answer. Definiteness is diagnosed term by term. A refuted condition is handled by a revision in step 3.

- **A condition can be asserted.** When the records hold no field for a condition, the researcher can state the condition in the state `established` or `refuted`, with pointers to the records or the publications that show it. The [types of problem](../../epistemology/objects/problem/problem-types.md) treat a conflict with a publication in the same way.
- **Representation is established through a binding.** When access is exercised on a model other than a [model that grounds the answer](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/conditions-of-answerability.md#^eight-conditions), the records show representation by a [binding](../../specification/binding.md#^binding-record) of that model to the phenomenon, whose computed comparison meets the required accuracy over the reference class, read under the [rules on the reports of a comparison](../../epistemology/objects/answer/satisfaction-conditions.md#^fidelity-reads-reports) that the warrant test of fidelity applies. When access is exercised on the target description, with no stand-in, representation holds trivially, and no binding is needed.
- **Once every condition of a route is in the state `established`, the acts that obtain the answer become ready.** The acts that exercise access obtain the quantity, a combination step composes the answer through the bridging inference, and the evaluation judges the obtained answer. When the answer rests on a model, the evaluation reads the comparison computed from the binding of that model, as the [model and its binding](../../epistemology/objects/answer/model-and-binding.md#^comparison) specify. That binding is the one through which access was exercised only when access ran on the model that grounds the answer. An interrogative act that obtains the answer to the question requires the soundness of that question, as the [rule on interrogative moves](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/conditions-of-answerability.md#^condition-dependencies) states.

## Step 3: act on each condition

### An open condition

The table lists, for each condition, the activities of the [registry](../../../src/gnomon/data/registries/activities.yml) that act on it, and marks as a *gap* each act that no registered activity types yet. An act moves a condition to the state `established` only through the record of its outcome, as the [states of a condition](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/conditions-of-answerability.md#^condition-states) require: its norm has passed, its outcome is positive, and that outcome has been accepted. ^condition-activities

| Condition | Activities that act on it | Gaps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definiteness | `explication`, `explicit-definition`, `operationalization`, `concept-revision`; `delimitation` and `formalization`, which write formal declarations | no activity produces a first concept from an explicandum; no activity raises a phenomenon to commitment `specified` |
| Answer type | `question-formulation`, which types the answer space; `delimitation`, which fixes the extent through the target | no activity fills or revises the fields of the epistemic task |
| Soundness | `phenomenon-detection`, for the topic presupposition; `derivation`, for an existence presupposition or a formal presupposition | no activity records a negative detection |
| Candidates | `abduction`, `induction`, `analogical-inference`, `causal-inference`, `decomposition-localization`, `model-analogy`, which propose candidates; `de-idealization`, `idealization`, `abstraction`, `formalization`, `fitting`, which revise a candidate; `derivation`, whose consequence enters a candidate as a law with a derived warrant; `critique`, which names objections; `admissibility-check`, which applies the admissibility tests | no activity builds a model from a target specification and a background without a phenomenon; no activity retrieves an accepted result as a candidate; no activity derives from a candidate together with the admitted laws, since `derivation` takes one record |
| Discrimination | `experimental-design`, whose norm requires that the possible outcomes discriminate the rival answers; `derivation`, `simulation`, `prediction`, `thought-experiment`, each supplying the value of a quantity under one candidate | no activity compares the consequences of rival candidates held as separate records |
| Access | `experimental-design`, whose design fixes the procedure; `operationalization` | no activity develops a proof technique or an algorithm |
| Bridging | `derivation` of a linking lemma; `background-appraisal`, which admits an auxiliary law; `operationalization`, whose scale a measurement model then validates | — |
| Representation | `validation`, which judges a model adequate for the purpose of the question through the comparison computed from its binding; `robustness-analysis`, `de-idealization`, `transfer`, `prediction`, and `testing` of a prediction of the model against the target | no activity writes the binding of a model to a phenomenon, or a key that bindings share |

Once every condition of a route is in the state `established`, the acts that obtain and evaluate the answer come next:

- the acts that exercise access: `measurement`, `observation`, `intervention`, `simulation`, and `derivation` along the route of construction;
- the acts that compose and evaluate the answer: `testing`, `confirmation`, `model-selection`, `inference-to-the-best-explanation`, `validation`, `critique`; `admissibility-check`, along the route of construction; `explanation`, `classification`, `unification`, `reduction` or `integration`, when the demand asks for their output type;
- the act that records the change of the accepted knowledge: `belief-revision`, once the researcher accepts or withdraws the answer, citing the verdict, the credence or the ranking on which that decision rests.

An act of evaluation that compares a model with the target produces no comparison of its own. The comparison is computed from a binding, a version of the data, and the metric and tolerance of the question, so the act reads it, and its verdict cites the inputs of the comparison.

An open condition can also become a question, as the [choice of the next act](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^condition-to-question) states. A demand that is a conjunction is decomposed by `question-decomposition`, with one question per conjunct linked by `subquestion-of`. When $Q_\pi$ has no question record yet, `question-formulation` first states a question schema, and `subject-identification` raises its slots, so that the record becomes a question.

Further gaps concern every route:

- no activity records the acceptance of a derived consequence, since `belief-revision` takes only a verdict, a credence or a ranking;
- no activity turns a derived consequence into a phenomenon;
- no activity records the acceptance of a verdict, which rests on the judgment of the norm of the step that produced it, and no activity judges the norm of an itinerary step;
- no activity produces the question into which an open condition turns, since `question-decomposition` requires erotetic implication;
- a question of explication has no record form, since its explicandum is not a slot.

### A refuted condition

The revisions of each refuted condition are listed in the theory of the [choice of the next act](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^refuted-condition), and the researcher chooses among them. No registered activity revises a question, narrows its contrast or the conditions that it holds fixed, or decomposes a problem into subproblems. After any revision that changes a diagnosed question, the procedure restarts from step 1.

## Step 4: readiness of an act

The criteria of [readiness](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^ready-act) are applied to each act instance found in step 3. Readiness reads only the steps that the itinerary records: a record whose producing step is absent from the itinerary carries no verdict that readiness could read.

## Step 5: ranking

The ready acts are ranked by the [ranking criteria](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^ranking-criteria), after the test of relevance removes the decorative ones. The order fixed by a rule, such as the order of the acts that follow a negative verdict, or the repair of a failed step before the act that rests on it, precedes the ranking.

## The acts named in practice

- **Raising a placeholder.** A slot is raised to `specified` on demand, as the [choice of the next act](../../epistemology/processes/inquiry/choice-of-act.md#^specified-on-demand) states. A candidate stated in the [answer form](../../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md) needs `specified` slots, since its claim, its grounds and its binding refer to declared symbols.
- **Refining the question.** A question is refined by the revisions of step 3: when the soundness of a generated question, the answer type, discrimination or representation is refuted, or when a concept is replaced after definiteness is refuted.
- **Specifying the form of the answer.** The answer type is one of the conditions. The full atomic question, its aim together with its parameters, fixes the type of its direct answers, and the epistemic task fixes that type as far as its fields are filled. A question left *open*, with no answer type yet, keeps this condition in the state `not established`. A field can be left open on purpose and narrowed later by a recorded revision. The proof obligations of the task constrain the bridging inference, and its required accuracy fixes the metric and the tolerance of the comparisons that judge representation and fidelity. The tests of success are drawn from the specifications of the question, as the [conditions of satisfaction](../../epistemology/objects/answer/satisfaction-conditions.md) state.
- **Decomposing.** A question is decomposed by `question-decomposition` when its demand is a conjunction. An open condition can also become a question, without an activity that types the move.
- **Deriving new claims.** A derivation acts on soundness for an existence presupposition or a formal presupposition, on discrimination through the consequences of one candidate, on bridging through a linking lemma, and on the candidates through a derived law. Along the route of construction, a derivation exercises access.

## Example

The facts below are those of the [records of the inquiry into delayed generalization](../../../examples/grokking-delayed-generalization/_index.md). The [problem](../../../examples/grokking-delayed-generalization/problem.yml) demands the determinants of the delay between fitting and generalization, a derivation of its duration, and its variation across random initializations. One [candidate answer](../../../examples/grokking-delayed-generalization/answer.yml) has status `hypothesized`. Read in the [answer form](../../epistemology/objects/answer/answer-form.md), it is an explanation whose claim is that the delay results from the slow structuring of the embedding representation, obtained by a derivation on a toy model of the networks. The answer record holds that model in its own blocks and states no binding. The question record `quest.grokking.explain_delay` addresses the problem, and the example takes it as the record of $Q_\pi$. This choice narrows the demand, since the record adds a contrast, the relation `mathematical` and the modal strength `actual`.

1. **Questions.** No direct answer is accepted, so the problem exists. The set $H$ holds one candidate and none is accepted, which axis A records as *unassessed candidates*, whether or not an assessment exists. Whether the accepted knowledge evokes $Q_\pi$, so that the problem has an epistemic gap, is the existence part of soundness, diagnosed in step 2. The questions to diagnose are $Q_\pi$ and the question of verdict on the candidate.
2. **Diagnosis.**
    - *The verdict.* The [assessment](../../../examples/grokking-delayed-generalization/assessment.yml) of the candidate is stabilized, and the assessment records `fail` for the admissibility tests of scope, grain and product, and for the warrant tests of closure, fidelity and robustness, while its admissibility test of admissible explanans is pending. In the [itinerary](../../../examples/grokking-delayed-generalization/itinerary.yml), the steps `check` and `test` that produced the assessment, by `admissibility-check` and by `testing`, both record the verdict `pending`. The negative verdict is therefore obtained, and it is not yet accepted.
    - *$Q_\pi$.* The answer type holds component by component: the aim `explanation`, the relation `mathematical` and the modal strength `actual` are filled in the question record, and the admissible explanans is left open on purpose. The admissibility test that reads it stays `pending`, so the condition of candidates can be opened but cannot reach the state `established` before that component is narrowed. The [phenomenon](../../../examples/grokking-delayed-generalization/phenomenon.yml) has the evidential status `established`, which shows that the topic presupposition holds, so the topic part of soundness is in the state `established`. The existence part, that an account of the demanded type exists, is in the state `not established`, since no record shows it, and the components that fix the type of result are filled, so the part is open. The condition of candidates is in the state `not established`, since no admissibility verdict on the candidate has been accepted with a positive outcome. The assessment records three failures among these tests. The admissibility test of scope fails because the range of the claim, derived from the hypotheses of its derivation, is a toy architecture, whereas the question ranges over finite overparameterized networks. The admissibility test of grain fails because the account does not derive the distribution of the delay across initializations. The evidence for the admissibility test of product cites both the class and the distribution. The admissibility test of modal strength passes, since the candidate claims the actual strength that the question requests, and whether that claim holds falls to the warrant tests, among which closure and fidelity fail. The answer type counts as fixed for the condition of candidates, so that condition is open.
3. **Acts.**
    - *The verdict, in order.* The norms of the steps `check` and `test` are judged first, an act that no registered activity types. The researcher then accepts the verdict, and the candidate is withdrawn by `belief-revision` or revised, as the researcher chooses.
    - *The soundness of $Q_\pi$.* The existence part is open. The registry maps `derivation` to it, for a proof that an account of the demanded type exists, and no such proof is in view, so the researcher can assert the part with pointers, or pose it as a question linked to $Q_\pi$ by `enables`.
    - *The candidates of $Q_\pi$.* One act proposes a rival: `abduction`, which takes the phenomenon record, and it does not rest on the verdict. Two acts would revise the candidate, so they are the third act of the rule on negative verdicts: they follow the acceptance of the verdict, and running them before it is one of the choices left to the researcher, their results then staying conditional on the verdict. For the class, `model-analogy` takes the toy model as its source, under the norm that the mapping preserves the relations relevant to the phenomenon and that no known difference defeats the transfer. `de-idealization` does not fit, since its norm requires a known correction to the idealized result, and no correction from the toy architecture to the class is known. For the distribution, a derivation must combine the candidate with the law of initialization, which the [background](../../../examples/grokking-delayed-generalization/background.yml) admits and the model of the candidate does not state, and no registered activity derives from a candidate together with the admitted laws.
4. **Readiness.**
    - `abduction` takes the phenomenon record as its input. That record was produced by the step `identify`, whose input descends from the steps `formulate` and `pose`, and all three verdicts are `pass`, so the act is ready. The itinerary records no step that raised the phenomenon to `specified`, so readiness reads only these verdicts.
    - `model-analogy` takes as its source model the model on which the candidate rests. The answer record holds that model in its own blocks, and that record was produced by the steps `propose` and `derive`, with the verdicts `pass` and `pending`, so the act is ready, and its result stays conditional on the verdict of `derive`.
    - The derivation of the distribution is blocked twice: its activity is a gap, and the background record was produced by the step `admit`, whose verdict is `fail`, so the repair of that step is a subgoal ranked before it.
5. **Ranking.** Within the verdict, the order is fixed by the rule on negative verdicts. The order of `abduction` relative to the acts on the verdict is left to the researcher, by the ranking criteria, whereas the two revisions of the candidate follow the acceptance of the verdict, unless the researcher runs them before it, conditionally. Within the revision for the distribution, the repair of the step `admit` precedes the derivation by the rule of readiness, not by the ranking.
