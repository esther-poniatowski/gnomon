# Taxonomic construction principles

These principles govern the construction of a classification, and each is drawn from a tradition of philosophy of science or epistemology. No tradition supplies an algorithm that yields a universally correct partition. The traditions converge instead on a demand for explicit warrant: each classificatory decision is tied to a declared function, tested against examples and counterexamples, and revised when it fails that function [chang2004].

The traditions bear on three questions about a classification:

- the **ontological status** of its classes: which kinds of entities populate the classification;
- the **epistemic criteria** that justify its partition;
- the **pragmatic constraints** that its function imposes, since a logical, explanatory, inductive, historical or institutional function each demands its own criteria of adequacy.

## Function and unit

- **Declared function.** A classification states what it serves: prediction, explanation, comparison, retrieval, communication, intervention, critique or theory-building. The function fixes the relevant similarity metric and the trade-offs that the classification may accept [hacking1999] [dupre1993].
- **Explicit unit of classification.** A classification states which objects it classifies, such as papers, concepts, methods, assumptions, models, phenomena or evidential roles, and classifies one kind of object at a time.
- **Approach fitted to the maturity of the field.** A conceptual typology is deductive and driven by theory, as Weber's ideal types are. An empirical taxonomy is inductive and driven by data, as numerical taxonomy is [sokal1963]. A hybrid approach refines a theoretical structure on data. The maturity of the field and the availability of measurable features decide among the three [bailey1994].

## Structure of the partition

- **Dimensions of variation before the partition.** A classification partitions a space of possible structures. The dimensions of that space are stated before the partition, which exposes the basis of each division (*fundamentum divisionis*) and prevents the conflation of distinct dimensions. The partition is then justified by the clustering that it induces in that space [suppes1960] [vanfraassen1980].
- **One basis of division per level.** Each level of a hierarchy subdivides along one dimension, and no new basis is introduced before the previous one is exhausted. A cross-classification is presented as orthogonal axes, not as nested levels. The conflation of two dimensions is the usual cause of overlapping or non-exhaustive categories [jevons1874] [iep_classification].
- **Exhaustive and exclusive levels.** At each level, every object of the domain falls in some category, $\bigcup_i C_i = \mathcal{D}$, and sibling categories do not overlap, $C_i \cap C_j = \emptyset$ for $i \neq j$, unless the overlap is declared. Kant's rules of logical division require members that exclude each other and whose union restores the divided concept [jevons1874] [iep_classification].
- **Positive and non-arbitrary classes.** A division follows a meaningful articulation of the domain, not a convenient opposition, and a class defined only negatively, such as "non-X", is avoided, since it defines no positive type [iep_classification].
- **Structural adequacy apart from empirical adequacy.** A classification can be exhaustive, exclusive and single-based yet empirically sterile, and conversely. The two adequacies are assessed independently.

## Warrant of a class

- **Inferential fruitfulness.** Membership in a class licenses projections to properties that the defining criterion does not contain, and these real kinds differ from nominal classes [mill1843]. A predicate supports generalization when it is projectible, and entrenchment in successful past projections contributes to projectibility [goodman1955]. Kinds are justified by the induction that they support, and a mature science replaces vague similarity by relations specific to its domain, such as phylogenetic proximity [quine1969]. Applied to a literature, a class of studies shares assumptions, methods, validity conditions and failure modes, beyond a shared vocabulary.
- **Mechanistic grounding.** A class whose properties co-occur because a causal mechanism sustains them is more projectible and better integrated into theory than a class of surface similarity. A homeostatic property cluster tolerates fuzzy boundaries and requires no single necessary and sufficient property, and its validity rests on three criteria: the cohesion of the cluster, its mechanistic grounding and its inductive yield [boyd1999]. Recent approaches ground kinds in their role in successful inquiry rather than in metaphysics [sep_natural_kinds].
- **Classification by generative mechanism.** Shared derived characters guide classification better than overall similarity, because a shared origin predicts projectible properties more reliably than surface resemblance [hennig1966]. Outside biology, a classification follows the generative mechanism or the causal etiology of its objects when that information is available.
- **Entrenchment weighed against revision.** An established category carries evidential weight from its past inductive success, so a departure from a received classification requires a justification proportional to the disruption that it introduces [goodman1955].

## Concepts and terms

- **Explication of terms.** A vague term of the literature is replaced by an explicatum that satisfies four desiderata: similarity to the explicandum, exactness, fruitfulness and simplicity, the last subject to the other three [carnap1950] [hempel1952]. The explicandum is first clarified through examples, counterexamples and contextual restrictions [sep_carnap_methodology]. Applied to a contested term, the explication records the observed uses, their ambiguities, the sharpened definition, the uses retained and excluded, and the reason for the restriction.
- **Graded membership when the phenomena require it.** A category unified by overlapping similarities has no common essence [wittgenstein1953], and many categories are organized around prototypes with graded membership [rosch1978]. A polythetic category admits a member that shares enough of a list of traits, such as the formulation of the problem, the explanatory aim, the formalism, the empirical protocol and the notion of evidence. The category declares this structure, and a strict operational definition replaces it when one is required.
- **Historical fidelity.** A vocabulary specific to a paradigm is preserved before it is translated, since a change of taxonomic structure can separate paradigms or neighboring subdisciplines [kuhn2000]. When two literatures use one term differently, the classification records the term used, its conceptual role, its background assumptions, its neighboring contrasts and its implicit ontology, before asserting any equivalence.

## Plurality and consequences

- **Plural classifications when warranted.** Several cross-cutting classifications of one domain can each be legitimate relative to distinct explanatory interests [dupre1993]. Consistency is required within each classification, not uniqueness across them. Applied to a literature, coordinated classifications by object, explanatory aim, method, theoretical commitment and level of analysis replace one overloaded tree, provided each states its function.
- **Revision by iteration.** A classification can start from an imperfect partition and improve by successive correction, and one official system may coordinate work while several research systems are tried [chang2004].
- **Reflexive audit.** Classifications act on the entities that they classify, at least among the human sciences [hacking2007], and classification systems shape action, visibility and coordination as infrastructures [bowker1999]. A classification that guides agendas, evaluation or disciplinary boundaries is therefore audited for the distinctions that it makes visible or hides, the research programs that it makes central or marginal, and the terms to which it gives normative authority.
