# Anomalous scaling in three-dimensional turbulence

## Research problem

Three-dimensional turbulence poses a theoretical problem about the statistics generated by the incompressible Navier–Stokes dynamics. Which dynamical constraints determine joint velocity-increment statistics at asymptotically high Reynolds number? Which parts of those statistics remain universal across forcing and boundary families?

## Formal target

The target comprises statistically stationary flows governed by the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. Velocity-increment distributions vary with spatial separation and with the orientation of the increment. Classical self-similar scaling and identical longitudinal and transverse intermittency provide substantive foils.

## Expressivity challenge

The later test must require the framework to:

- treat the governing equations as complete formal laws without treating their statistical consequences as analytically solved;
- represent a distribution-valued observable followed across spatial scale and an ensemble of flow realizations;
- place the regime where the Reynolds number tends to infinity in the domain because the question concerns the limit system itself;
- keep changes in forcing and boundaries available for a test of the claimed universality range;
- combine an explanatory aim with a mathematical relation and an explicitly delimited necessity claim, and hold intelligibility and structural transparency constitutive of the product rather than preferred;
- distinguish structural accuracy from numerical accuracy through their respective targets: the joint increment distribution and the two exponent families.

## Scope boundary

The case assumes neither universal exponent values nor a closed turbulence model. A candidate answer must state the conditions under which any universality claim applies.

## Scientific basis

A perturbative multiplier framework derives anomalous exponents in a shell model ([mailybaev2026](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/6qrr-3646)). The derivation supplies an analytic route for an idealized cascade model and does not establish the result for three-dimensional Navier–Stokes flow. Longitudinal and transverse velocity gradients exhibit distinct intermittent statistics in direct numerical simulations, so their disparity remains part of the problem to explain ([buaria2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/turbulence-intermittency-and-velocity-gradients/83110A5131F55CF9B890415703AA7770)).
